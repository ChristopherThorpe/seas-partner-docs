# Approach C: a course master agreement with project schedules

## Summary

Each offering of a course has one Course Project Master Agreement. Every student and every member of the Course Staff signs its Signature Roster once. A partner joins it for one project by signing that project's Project Schedule, which has a Partner part naming the partner and its Partner Contacts. The master states every rule once: the course-wide rules; the Course Staff; the three information choices; the three ownership choices, with one complete subsection each; the public licenses; publication review and the patent notice; teammates' rights and the bracketed buyout; Harvard; and the general terms. Each project, with or without a partner, has one Project Schedule of about two pages: its choices, with defaults printed beside them, the instructor-approval certification, and the signatures of its students, its partner, and any Course Staff who need Confidential Information. Because everyone is a party to one instrument, students are bound to each other and to every partner directly, so the course-wide protections need no third-party-beneficiary lists and no "as if a Participant" cross-references. Each partner's rights and duties are limited to its own project, plus the course-wide terms that protect partners' information (master Section 1.4). Harvard approves the templates once, and the approval is kept on file.

## Documents and length

Word counts are approximate. "Agreement text" excludes notes for counsel, form blanks, and check boxes.

| File | What it is | Agreement text | Notes for counsel | Whole file |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `master-agreement.md` | The Course Project Master Agreement, with Course details and the Signature Roster | 6,360 | 700 | 7,300 |
| `project-schedule.md` | The form for each project, with or without a partner; a partner joins by signing it | 700 | none | 860 |
| `harvard-approval.md` | Harvard's one-time approval of the templates for listed courses | 360 | 130 | 520 |

The owner accepted a master of about 6,000 words of agreement text, because it holds every substantive term for both course-wide and project-level matters, which approach A splits between two documents. The review estimated that its cuts would save about 240 words, but its fixes added more, so the master is about 360 words over that figure.

In the citations below, "MA" is the master agreement, "PS" the Project Schedule, and "HA" the Harvard approval.

## Strengths

- **One instrument, direct promises.** Students are parties to each other and to every partner, so the rules protecting other projects (MA 3) are direct promises. There is no third-party-beneficiary list (v0.5 §11.7) and no Exhibit D re-applying sections "as if a Participant".
- **One form per project.** The master defines every choice once. A project's paperwork is one schedule, which a partner joins by signing.
- **Same form for every project.** The Project Schedule works with or without a partner, for an individual or a team.
- **Joining, leaving, and reassignment are simple.** Everyone has signed the master before choosing a project, so work done before signing a schedule is already covered (MA 3.8), and a late joiner signs one block.
- **Stable terms.** The master cannot change during the offering (MA 11.1). Only a schedule changes, and only by its own signers (MA 11.2), so no partner's deal is affected by another's. A partner is bound by no other project's schedule (MA 12.9).
- **Course Staff sign once.** The Signature Roster binds every TA and instructor to the course-wide rules, which resolves review note R-4. Harvard approves once (HA).

## Weaknesses

- **Partners' likely reaction to a multi-party master.** A company is asked to join one agreement with every student in the course and with other companies, some of them possibly competitors. Its lawyer will ask what it owes the other parties, whether there is antitrust exposure, whether it is bound to companies that join later, and who the other parties are. Its compliance team may want to screen counterparties it cannot see. MA 1.4, 10.4, 12.9, 12.10, and 12.11 answer these points, but the structure invites the questions. Some partners will prefer a bilateral agreement (approaches A and B). The reviewer suggests testing the structure with two or three real partners' counsel before choosing it.
- **The whole master must be read, or at least its partner sections.** A partner's lawyer must review about two-thirds of a 6,000-word master. The reading map above helps.
- **No negotiation, and no mid-offering corrections.** A partner that needs a different term cannot get it in its schedule. An error that counsel finds during the term stays until the next offering (open question 2).
- **Proving that a student is bound.** Partners do not see the Signature Roster. A partner gets a copy of a named student's entry only on request about a suspected breach, under the consent on the Roster (MA 12.11), which counsel must confirm meets FERPA.
- **Students sign early.** Students sign a long agreement, much of it about partner projects, before they know which project they will join.
- **Length.** The master is about 360 words over the accepted figure of 6,000.

## Open questions

### For the owner

1. **A Student Company acquired by a competitor.** The competitor test is applied when the company first uses the work or receives an assignment (MA 6.4), as v0.5 decided. A competitor of Partner can then buy a Student Company and keep its Teammate License and, under joint ownership, its exemption from the FSL's competing-use bar. Should a later acquisition by a competitor end that license or exemption? "Competitor" is also undefined, as in v0.5.
2. **Corrections during an offering.** The reviewer suggested letting a project's parties adopt a correction to the master that Harvard has approved. This draft keeps the master fixed (MA 11.1), because register decision B5 allows no amendment other than options before the Course ends.
3. **TAs who sign a schedule.** They must sign every changed schedule (MA 11.2) and consent to sharing in a public forum (MA 7.1), as register decision B5 and v0.5 §5.1 require. The reviewer suggested that they sign only changes to the information choice. That would change B5, so it is not drafted.
4. **Leavers' rights in later work** (MA 6.6). This narrows the brief's license "to the team's Project Work Product" for a student who leaves. Please confirm.
5. **Knowing whether information is Course Private** (MA 3.4). The duty now applies to information a person knows, or has reason to know, is Course Private. The reviewer also suggested that the Course Staff tell the Course Community which projects chose Course Private. That would be a new duty, so it is not drafted; labeling Course Private materials is a practical alternative.

### For counsel

These match the notes for counsel in the documents.

1. **Multi-party formation, antitrust, and counterparty screening** (MA 1.3, 1.4).
2. **FERPA consent on the Roster** (MA 4.3, 12.11): does it meet 34 C.F.R. § 99.30?
3. **SEAS community and attendees** (MA 3.2; R-8), including whether partners' representatives at events should owe a duty.
4. **Course Staff contributions** (MA 4.2; HA): Harvard may own them.
5. **Co-ownership law** (MA 6.8; R-2, R-7), equal interests, and later joiners' interests.
6. **Patent risk at poster sessions** (MA 7.5).
7. **Buyout** (MA 8.3): 17 U.S.C. § 204(a) and 35 U.S.C. § 261; the power of attorney; adhesion and unconscionability; payment by promise and reversion; securities law; tax, including 26 U.S.C. § 1221(a)(3); and the profit-sharing waiver's reach to heirs, creditors, and foreign law.
8. **Limit on liability** (MA 10.4): the confidentiality exception, and claims between students.
9. **Harvard and Course Staff protection** (MA 10.5; R-5, R-6).
10. **Harvard's approval** (HA): the wording of the exception, enforcement of MA 10.5 from an approval on file, and which office signs.

## Decisions the drafter made that the brief did not settle

Items marked "(owner)" were decided by the owner after review, and items marked "(review)" follow the reviewer's recommendation.

1. **A partner joins by signing the project's schedule** (owner). The Partner Joinder is merged into the PS's Partner part. A partner with two projects signs each schedule separately and is a separate Partner for each (MA 1.4).
2. **Roster privacy and proof** (owner). Partners do not receive the Roster or other projects' schedules. Each student consents on the Roster to the Course instructors giving a partner, on request about a suspected breach of Section 3, a copy of the student's signed entry, without any personal email (MA 12.11). The Roster also takes an optional personal email for later notices (MA 12.2).
3. **The master is fixed for the offering** (MA 11.1). After the Duration, a project's signers may agree to other terms that affect no one else. A result other than Open Science still needs approval (MA 11.3; owner).
4. **Only current signers sign a changed schedule** (MA 6.6, 11.2). Otherwise one departed student could block any change, or have their rights changed without consent.
5. **Effect of a change** (MA 11.2). A new information choice applies to information given afterwards. A new ownership choice covers earlier work of those who sign, and replaces the licenses among them, unless the schedule says otherwise. Partner takes title subject to public licenses already granted, and to the Teammate Licenses of students who left before the change (review).
6. **Leavers get no rights in later work** (MA 6.6), apart from public licenses. The owner is asked to confirm this (open question 4).
7. **Joint ownership covers software only** (MA 6.8). Everything else is treated as under Students own, following v0.5 §8.1.1. FSL-1.1-MIT is the default FSL version (MA 6.1).
8. **Defaults for unusable or unapproved choices** (MA 2.1, 2.2). A choice the project cannot use gets the default, except that Course Private and Confidential without a partner means Course Private (review). A choice that needs approval but has none recorded gets its default (review).
9. **Every individual project needs approval, with or without a partner** (MA 2.2; owner). With a partner, Partner certifies it on the PS; without one, the student does. The shared Student Guide already says this.
10. **Teammate License** (MA 8.2). It has the same scope as Partner's license, including patent rights. Under joint ownership, a Teammate License in the code is subject to the FSL limits (owner). It waives any share of profits between teammates. Ordinary end-user terms for customers are not sublicensing, for Partner or teammates (MA 6.7(c); review).
11. **Buyout mechanics** (MA 8.3; owner). Notice goes to the harvard.edu address and the last known personal email, and the first notice governs. The present assignment takes effect on payment or tender of the price, or, under Alternative 2, on a signed promise to pay. The company must first agree to be bound. A power of attorney covers confirming documents after 30 days. Equity that is refused may be replaced by its fair market value in cash. Alternative 1's price measure is bracketed as the [lowest] or [average] value a founder received (review).
12. **Who else may enforce** (MA 12.10; owner). A Student Company may enforce MA 6.8, 8.2, and 8.3 as they benefit it, and recipients of a public license have that license's rights.
13. **Transfers of ownership interests** (MA 12.3; review). A student may transfer an interest only to someone who agrees in writing to be bound, so the profit-sharing waiver and other terms follow the work.
14. **Course Staff contributions** (MA 4.2; review). A TA must not contribute. If a TA does, the TA grants a non-exclusive license so far as the TA can, and Harvard's possible ownership is flagged.
15. **Telling "the Course Staff if the sender is unknown"** (MA 3.3), for a found folder whose owner is unclear (scenario 19).
16. **Any party entitled to enforce a term may seek an injunction** (MA 12.4), so other partners' and teammates' enforcement rights have an equitable remedy.
17. **End Dates** (MA 2.5). For course-wide duties, a person's End Date is when they leave the Course. A Partner's End Date is always the end of the Duration.
18. **Harvard's-rights boxes are in the master's Course details**, because the master is per offering (scenario 22).
19. **Course Private without a partner binds the team too** (MA 5.4); otherwise the choice would not protect the team from its own members.
20. **The master is the course policy** (MA 12.9). v0.5's choice between Exhibits A.ES and A.DS is dropped.
21. **Publication review under Partner owns covers disclosures about the Project Work Product** (MA 7.4), not only those about Partner Information as in v0.5 §5.2, so that Partner can protect patentable work it owns (review).
22. **A knowledge standard for other projects' Course Private Information** (MA 3.4; review). See open question 5.
23. **A schedule that names a partner binds no one until that partner signs** (MA 2.4; review), so students are not bound to license or assign to a company that never joins.
24. **Joint ownership details are bracketed** (MA 6.8(a); review): whether interests are equal, and whether later joiners take interests in earlier code.
25. **The liability placeholder offers "[or to another student]"** (MA 10.4; review), because students are now parties to each other.
26. **The entire agreement is defined project by project** (MA 12.9; review). A partner is bound by no other project's schedule, and "together they form one agreement" is dropped (MA 12.6).
27. **"Course instructors will honor" and "will give"** (MA 3.8, 12.11; review), following the brief's wording, so individual TAs are not personally answerable for course administration.
28. **A Partner acts through any authorized officer** (MA 1.4, 12.2; review), not only the one who signed, so changes still work after staff turnover or an acquisition.
29. **The Course Staff section follows Section 3** (review), so the course-wide terms sit together.
30. **The schedule carries a version number**, and Partner's acknowledgment of a later joiner is optional, because joining is not a change.

## Review findings skipped or adjusted

The review's 37 findings were each checked against the text, and all were accurate. The following were not applied as written.

- **Finding 1 (buyout):** applied as the owner specified. The reviewer's reversion if the company never pays is raised in the counsel note rather than drafted.
- **Finding 3 (FSL):** Teammate Licenses in the code are made subject to the FSL limits (owner), rather than excluded.
- **Finding 4 (competitor test):** the timing is kept (owner); see open question 1.
- **Finding 11:** "without use of it" (owner), rather than "without use of or reference to it".
- **Finding 14:** the knowledge standard is applied. The duty on the Course Staff to announce which projects chose Course Private is skipped, as a new duty that the knowledge standard makes unnecessary (open question 5).
- **Finding 23 (corrections):** skipped, because it would change register decision B5 (open question 2).
- **Finding 28 (TA signatures):** the "will honor" wording is applied. Limiting TAs to signing information changes is skipped, because it would change register decision B5 (open question 3).
- **Finding 35:** the Partner Joinder fix is moot, as the joinder is merged into the schedule.
- **Finding 37 (simplicity):** the merge and cuts are applied, with two adjustments. The old 5.9(d) and (e) became one cross-reference paragraph (MA 6.9(d)), so each ownership subsection stays complete. The license menu was cut from MA 6.7(b), but its defaults are kept there. The reading map is in this README, because the Student Guide is shared and not edited here, and the partner cover email is outside these documents.
