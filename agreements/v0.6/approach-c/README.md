# Approach C: a course master agreement with project schedules

## Summary

Each offering of a course has one Course Project Master Agreement. Every student and every member of the Course Staff signs its Signature Roster once, and each partner joins it for its own project by signing a one-page Partner Joinder. The master states every rule once: the course-wide rules; the three information choices; the three ownership choices, with one complete subsection each; the public licenses; publication review and the patent notice; teammates' rights and the bracketed buyout; the Course Staff; Harvard; and the general terms. Each project, with or without a partner, then has a Project Schedule of one to two pages: its choices, with defaults printed beside them, the instructor-approval certification, and the signatures of its students, its partner, and any Course Staff who need Confidential Information. Because everyone is a party to one instrument, students are bound to each other and to every partner directly, so the course-wide protections need no third-party-beneficiary lists and no "as if a Participant" cross-references. Each partner's rights and duties are limited to its own project, plus the few course-wide terms that protect partners' information (master Section 1.4). Harvard approves the templates once, and the approval is kept on file.

## Documents and length

Word counts are approximate. "Agreement text" excludes notes for counsel, form blanks, and check boxes.

| File | What it is | Agreement text | Notes for counsel | Whole file |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `master-agreement.md` | The Course Project Master Agreement, with Course details and the Signature Roster | 5,900 | 430 | 6,570 |
| `partner-joinder.md` | One-page form by which a partner joins for one project and names its Partner Contacts | 280 | none | 340 |
| `project-schedule.md` | One- to two-page form for each project, with or without a partner | 630 | none | 770 |
| `harvard-approval.md` | Harvard's one-time approval of the templates for listed courses | 350 | 110 | 500 |

The master is about 400 words over the 5,500-word target. It holds every substantive term for both course-wide and project-level matters, which approach A splits between a Course Agreement and a Project Agreement. The joinder and schedule are short because they only record choices and signatures.

In the citations below, "MA" is the master agreement, "PS" the Project Schedule, "PJ" the Partner Joinder, and "HA" the Harvard approval.

## Strengths

- **One instrument, direct promises.** Students are parties to each other and to every partner, so the rules protecting other projects (MA 3) are direct promises. There is no third-party-beneficiary list (v0.5 §11.7) and no Exhibit D re-applying sections "as if a Participant".
- **Each decision stated once.** The master defines every choice once; a project's paperwork is a page of check boxes plus a one-page joinder.
- **Same form for every project.** One Project Schedule works with or without a partner, for an individual or a team.
- **Joining, leaving, and reassignment are simple.** Everyone has signed the master before choosing a project, so work done before signing a schedule is already covered (MA 3.8), and a late joiner signs one block.
- **Stable terms.** The master cannot change during the offering (MA 11.1). Only a schedule changes, and only by its own signers (MA 11.2), so no partner's deal is affected by another's.
- **Course Staff sign once.** The Signature Roster binds every TA and instructor to the course-wide rules, which resolves review note R-4. Harvard approves once (HA).

## Weaknesses

- **Partners' likely reaction to a multi-party master.** A company is asked to join one agreement with every student in the course and with other companies, some of them possibly competitors. Its lawyer will ask what it owes the other parties, whether there is antitrust exposure, whether it is bound to companies that join later, and who the other parties are. Sections 1.4, 10.4, 12.10, and 12.11 answer these points, but the structure invites the questions. Some partners, especially those with standard NDA practices, will prefer a bilateral agreement (approaches A and B).
- **The whole master must be read.** A partner's lawyer must review about 6,000 words, including terms that never apply to its project, such as the teammates' buyout and projects without partners.
- **No negotiation.** A partner that needs a different term cannot get it in its schedule. It must wait for the next offering, or not sponsor. v0.5 also barred changes during the Course, but a separate agreement per project would be easier to vary later.
- **No mid-offering corrections.** An error that counsel finds during the term stays until the next offering, because amending the master would need every party.
- **Partners cannot see the other parties.** The Signature Roster may be an education record under FERPA, so it is withheld from partners (MA 12.11). Enforcing Section 3 against a student on another project then needs the Course Staff to confirm that the student signed (bracketed in MA 12.11).
- **Students sign early.** Students sign a long agreement, much of it about partner projects, before they know which project they will join.
- **Length.** The master is about 400 words over the target.
- **Student Guide wording.** The shared guide describes the documents generically, including a "short written election" for projects without a partner. If approach C is chosen, the guide should name the Master Agreement and Project Schedule instead.

## Open questions

These are the notes for counsel in the documents, plus questions that the structure raises.

1. **Formation and privity (MA 1.3).** Is each party bound to parties who sign later, without further action? Is consideration adequate between, for example, a student and a partner on another project?
2. **Antitrust (MA 1.4 note).** Is a common agreement among competing partners a concern, given that it restricts no competition and passes no information between them?
3. **FERPA and the roster (MA 12.11).** May partners see the Signature Roster? If not, is the bracketed confirmation by the Course Staff acceptable?
4. **Partners at Private Course Events (MA 3.2 note).** As in v0.5, partners' representatives owe no duty to keep other projects' Course Private Information private. Should the master add one, despite partners' likely resistance?
5. **Limit on liability (MA 10.4).** Should ordinary confidentiality breaches be excepted? The limit also runs between partners.
6. **Harvard and Course Staff protection (MA 10.5; R-5, R-6).** Which carve-outs apply, and should intentional misuse in breach of Section 3 also lose the protection?
7. **SEAS community (MA 3.2; R-8).** Who is included, and should attendees be told not to record?
8. **Co-ownership law (MA 5.8 note; R-2, R-7).** Please confirm the U.S. rules, and that the joint licensor can enforce the FSL license.
9. **Buyout (MA 7.4 note).** Enforceability, securities law, and tax; which price alternative; whether the sale ends the seller's license; and whether to add a time limit.
10. **Teammates' profit-sharing waiver (MA 7.2).** Is it effective, and does it displace a co-owner's duty to account?
11. **Patent risk at poster sessions (MA 6.5 note).** Where Partner owns, should Partner get copies [10] business days before a Private Course Event?
12. **Harvard's approval (HA note).** Is Harvard's statement of the exception accurate, and is an approval kept on file enough for Harvard to enforce MA 10.5?
13. **Errata process.** Should Harvard be able to approve a correction to the master mid-offering that reduces no party's rights? This draft does not allow it, because partners' lawyers would resist a non-party's power to change their contract.

## Decisions the drafter made that the brief did not settle

1. **One Partner Joinder per project.** A partner with two projects signs two joinders and is a separate Partner for each (MA 1.4). This keeps each partner's rights and duties within one project.
2. **The authorized officer signs both the joinder and the schedule.** The schedule holds the partner's choices and its certification of instructor approval, and changes to it need the officer anyway (decision A6). Both go in one signing envelope.
3. **Partners do not receive the roster or other projects' documents** (MA 12.11; PJ 4). This protects students' enrollment information and other partners' identities. A bracketed sentence lets the Course Staff confirm, on request, whether a named person signed.
4. **The master is fixed for the offering** (MA 11.1). After the Duration, a project's signers may agree to other terms that affect no one else (MA 11.3). This carries v0.5 §11.4 into a multi-party setting.
5. **Only current signers sign a changed schedule.** A student whose End Date has passed need not sign, and their contributions stay under the terms in force when they left (MA 5.6, 11.2). Otherwise one departed student could block any change, or have their rights changed without consent.
6. **Effect of a change** (MA 11.2). A new information choice applies to information given afterwards. A new ownership choice covers earlier work of those who sign, and replaces the licenses among them, unless the schedule says otherwise. Public licenses for released copies stay. This answers scenario 12, and gives Partner clean title after an agreed assignment.
7. **Leavers get no rights in later work** (MA 5.6), apart from public licenses. A student who leaves in week 2 should not receive a perpetual license to the finished product.
8. **Joint ownership covers software only** (MA 5.8). Everything else is treated as under Students own, with the Partner's license and the chosen public license, following v0.5 §8.1.1.
9. **FSL-1.1-MIT is the default** when joint ownership is chosen but no FSL version is checked (MA 5.1; PS C), because MIT is the default elsewhere.
10. **A choice the project cannot use gets the default** (MA 2.1). Examples are Partner owns without a partner, and Course Private and Confidential without a partner. This extends the brief's rule for blank or doubled choices, as v0.5 Exhibit D did.
11. **"An individual project always needs approval"** is read as applying to projects without a partner (MA 2.2), as in v0.5 G4. A single student on a partner project needs approval only for choices other than Open Science.
12. **The Teammate License has the same scope as Partner's license**, including patent rights. It expressly covers products and services sold to customers, and it waives any share of profits (MA 7.2). Other sublicensing needs the grantor's consent. A Student Company must be able to serve customers, and the Student Guide's counsel note asks whether royalty-free licenses displace the duty to account; the waiver is flagged for counsel.
13. **The buyout is self-executing** (MA 7.4(c)). The assignment takes effect when the period ends and the price is paid or promised in writing, and the company may sign confirming documents as attorney-in-fact. This handles a holdout who "won't sign anything" (scenario 8), and is flagged for counsel. The buyout leaves Partner's license, other teammates' rights, and the seller's portfolio use untouched (MA 7.4(f)).
14. **Course Staff claim no rights in Project Work Product, and their suggestions may be used freely** (MA 8.2). This follows v0.5 Exhibit A.DS Section 3, and keeps title clear if a TA contributes anyway (scenario 24).
15. **Tell "the Course Staff if the sender is unknown"** (MA 3.3), for a found folder whose owner is unclear (scenario 19).
16. **Any party entitled to enforce a term may seek an injunction** (MA 12.4), not only the project's Partner. Otherwise other partners' and teammates' enforcement rights would have no equitable remedy.
17. **End Dates.** For course-wide duties, a person's End Date is when they leave the Course. A Partner's End Date is the end of the Duration (MA 2.5). The brief ties the Confidentiality Period to an End Date, and these duties are not tied to one project.
18. **Harvard's-rights boxes are in the master's Course details**, because the master is per offering and the boxes describe the offering (scenario 22).
19. **Course Private without a partner binds the team too.** Each student must keep the team's information within the Course Community unless all agree otherwise (MA 4.5); otherwise the choice would not protect the team from its own members.
20. **The master is the course policy** (MA 1.5). v0.5's choice between Exhibits A.ES and A.DS is dropped. The binding acknowledgments are in the master and the Roster, and the explanation is in the Student Guide.
21. **The event notice is not in the text.** The idea of telling Private Course Event attendees not to record is raised in the R-8 note rather than imposed on the Course Staff.
22. **The schedule carries a version number** (PS "Version"), to track changes. Partner's acknowledgment of a later joiner is optional (PS "Seen by Partner"), because joining is not a change.
