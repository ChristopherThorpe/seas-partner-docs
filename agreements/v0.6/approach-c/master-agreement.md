## 1. How this agreement works

**1.1 Parties.** This course project master agreement (this "**Master Agreement**") governs projects in the course offering named above (the "**Course**", including every course in a listed sequence) at the Harvard John A. Paulson School of Engineering and Applied Sciences ("**SEAS**"). The "**Duration**" is every term and session of that offering. The parties are:

- each student in the Course, and each instructor, teaching assistant, teaching fellow, course assistant, or other member of its teaching staff (together, the "**Course Staff**"), who signs the signature roster at the end (the "**Signature Roster**"); and
- each organization that signs a partner joinder in the approved form (a "**Partner Joinder**") for a project, as that project's "**Partner**".

President and Fellows of Harvard College ("**Harvard**") is not a party.

**1.2 Projects.** Each project in the Course (a "**Project**") has one project schedule in the approved form (a "**Schedule**"), which records its choices and blanks. A Project has one Partner or none. The students who sign a Project's Schedule are its "**Project Students**", and each is a "**teammate**" of the others. The Project Students and any Course Staff who sign the Schedule are its "**Project Signers**". "**Project Work Product**" means all results, reports, presentations, posters, videos, software, data, designs, and other work product that any Project Student creates, alone or with others, in the course of the Project.

**1.3 One agreement, separate Projects.** Each party agrees with every other party, including those who sign later, in exchange for the others' promises and the chance to take part in the Course's Projects. A Project's terms bind and benefit only its Partner and Project Signers, except where this Master Agreement says otherwise.

**1.4 Partners.** A Partner is a party only for its own Project. Its rights and duties are those that this Master Agreement gives a Partner for its Project, plus the course-wide terms that bind or protect partners: Sections 3.3, 3.4, 3.7, 8.3, 10.4, and 10.5. Partners owe each other nothing else, and no Partner has any right in another Project. A Partner that sponsors two Projects signs a Partner Joinder for each. A Partner acts through the authorized officer who signs its Partner Joinder and Schedule, and through the individuals its Partner Joinder names as its "**Partner Contacts**".

> **Note for counsel:** A partner's lawyer will likely ask whether joining an agreement with other companies, possibly competitors, creates duties or liability toward them (Sections 1.4 and 10.4 aim to prevent this); whether it raises antitrust concerns (it restricts no competition and passes no information between Partners); whether each party is bound to later signers without further action (Section 1.3); and who the other parties are. The Signature Roster may be an education record under FERPA, so Section 12.11 keeps it from Partners. Please confirm each point.

**1.5 Course policy.** This Master Agreement is the Course's policy on intellectual property and confidentiality for projects, and it governs if another Course document conflicts with it. Each Partner agrees to restrict information only as far as bona fide business needs require.

**1.6 Coursework.** Each Project is an educational activity of the Course, and its supervision and grading are solely the Course Staff's responsibility. A Partner may benefit from the results. No Partner employs or pays any student or member of the Course Staff, and none of them may bind a Partner. No one must offer or accept employment, and this Master Agreement creates no agency, partnership, or fiduciary relationship.

## 2. Choosing, joining, and leaving a Project

**2.1 Open Science is the default.** An "**Open Science**" project is one whose Schedule has, by choice or by default: Open information; Students own; the MIT License, the Apache License 2.0, or both, for software; and CC BY 4.0 or CC BY-SA 4.0 for everything else.

**2.2 Instructor approval.** Any other choice needs a Course instructor's approval in writing (email is enough), at signing and for any later change. Partner certifies that approval on the Schedule; without a Partner, the Project Students do. An individual project (one Project Student) without a Partner always needs it. The instructor does not sign.

**2.3 Before starting.** Before choosing a Project, each student must read the Course's student guide (the "**Student Guide**") and confirm on the Signature Roster that they have. The Student Guide explains this Master Agreement but binds no one. A student may ask to be assigned to an Open Science project or a Project without a Partner. Each student must sign a Project's Schedule before receiving its Partner's information or starting material work on it.

**2.4 Joining.** Each person is bound from the date they sign, and a Schedule also covers information given to its Project before then. A student who joins the Course late signs the Signature Roster then. Anyone who joins a Project later signs the joinder block on its Schedule; joining is not a change. A Project's choices apply to all its Project Students alike.

**2.5 End Date and Confidentiality Period.** A person's "**End Date**" for a Project is the earlier of the end of the Duration and the date they stop taking part in it, including by withdrawing from the Course. For Section 3, the date they leave the Course counts instead. A Partner's End Date is the end of the Duration. Every duty under this Master Agreement to protect information lasts until five years after the person's End Date (the "**Confidentiality Period**"), and for a trade secret, as long as it remains one.

**2.6 Capstone.** If the Course is a capstone for a student's degree, the student may decline every partner Project and do a Project without a Partner. The student was also offered the choice of writing a thesis instead.

## 3. Rules for everyone in the Course

**3.1 Scope.** This Section 3 binds every student and member of the Course Staff, whether or not they sign a Schedule, and protects every Project. Sections 3.3 and 3.4 also bind Partners. Partners may enforce this Section 3 for their own information, and Project Students for their Project's Course Private Information.

**3.2 Definitions.**

- The "**Course Community**" is (a) students enrolled in the Course during the Duration; (b) the Course Staff; (c) Harvard faculty and staff who support the Course; and (d) [the students, faculty, and staff of SEAS] and invited guests attending a Private Course Event, while attending it.
- A "**Private Course Event**" is a class session, presentation, poster session, video showcase, project review, or similar Course event that is not open to the public and is limited to [the students, faculty, and staff of SEAS] and invited guests.
- "**Partner Information**" is all information, in any form, that a Partner, or anyone acting for it, gives or makes available for its Project.
- "**Course Private Information**" may be shared only within the Course Community. It is Partner Information when its Project's Schedule so provides, and a Project's own information when a Project without a Partner so chooses.
- "**Confidential Information**" is Partner Information that its Partner marks under Section 4.4. It may be shared only with its Partner and its Project's Project Signers.
- Course Private Information and Confidential Information are together "**Restricted Information**".

[At each Private Course Event, the Course Staff must tell attendees not to photograph, record, or share Course Private Information outside the Course Community.]

> **Note for counsel (review note R-8):** Please settle who counts as the SEAS community (for example, cross-registered and Extension School students, postdocs, visiting scholars, other Harvard schools, and alumni) and how membership is shown. The bracketed notice is one way to tell attendees that material must stay private.

**3.3 Inadvertent Disclosures.** An "**Inadvertent Disclosure**" occurs when anyone's confidential information reaches someone in the Course who is not authorized to receive it, for example through a mistaken file or a sharing error. Any student, member of the Course Staff, or Partner who receives one must use reasonable care to (a) tell the sender, or the Course Staff if the sender is unknown; (b) not copy, forward, or otherwise use it; and (c) delete every copy, including notes and photos. No one is liable for merely receiving an Inadvertent Disclosure, but that protection does not cover intentional use or disclosure afterwards. Taking steps (a) to (c) promptly after learning of it is reasonable care. Course Private Information shared within the Course Community is not an Inadvertent Disclosure.

**3.4 Other Projects' Course Private Information.** Each student and member of the Course Staff must keep the Course Private Information of any Project whose Schedule they have not signed within the Course Community, use it only for the Course, and never pass it to another Partner. This does not apply where that Project's Partner and Project Students (or, without a Partner, its Project Students) consent in writing. No Partner may ask anyone for another Project's Restricted Information without that consent.

**3.5 Others' confidential information.** No student or member of the Course Staff may share with anyone in the Course, or use in a Project, anyone else's confidential information that they are not authorized to share or use, such as a current or former employer's.

**3.6 Generative AI.** Students must use their Harvard accounts and Harvard-provided generative AI tools for Course work, unless the Course Staff allow otherwise or a Partner asks its Project to use the Partner's tools at its expense. A Partner's Restricted Information may be used only with generative AI tools that the Partner approves in writing (email is enough), which may include Harvard's.

**3.7 Exclusions and permitted disclosures.** No duty to protect information applies to information that the recipient can show is or becomes public through no breach, that they knew without restriction before receiving it, that they received from a third party with no duty of confidentiality, or that they developed independently. Anyone may disclose information as a law, regulation, subpoena, or court order requires, after giving its owner prompt notice where permitted, and reasonable help at the owner's expense to seek a protective order. Nothing prevents reporting a possible violation of law to a government agency, or any other legally protected disclosure. Under 18 U.S.C. § 1833(b), no individual is liable under trade secret law for disclosing a trade secret in confidence to a government official or an attorney solely to report or investigate a suspected violation of law, or in a court filing made under seal.

**3.8 Reassignment.** A student may ask to move to another Project, including an Open Science project or a Project without a Partner, and the Course Staff must honor the request. Work the student did on the first Project stays under its terms, as if the student had signed its Schedule. The student must keep the first Partner's information confidential as Course Private Information, or more strictly if that Project's terms require. That Project's Partner and Project Signers may enforce this Section 3.8.

## 4. Information choices

**4.1 The choices.** Each Schedule chooses Open (the default), Course Private, or Course Private and Confidential. The last needs a Partner.

**4.2 Open.** Partner must not give any Restricted Information, or any personal data, third-party data, or other data restricted by law or contract ("**Protected Data**"). Nothing about the Project is confidential except as Section 3 requires, but Partner is still masked where Section 6 requires.

**4.3 Course Private.** All Partner Information is Course Private Information. If Partner gives something by mistake and identifies it as confidential, each Project Signer must, when Partner asks in writing, delete it, stop using it, and make reasonable efforts to remove it from submitted coursework. Earlier uses that were permitted for Course Private Information are not a breach.

**4.4 Course Private and Confidential.** All Partner Information is Course Private Information unless Partner clearly marks it as Confidential Information: in writing when it is given or, for an oral or visual disclosure, at the time, with written confirmation within ten business days. Partner may give Confidential Information only to Project Signers. No one may give it to a member of the Course Staff who has not signed the Schedule.

**4.5 Without a Partner.** If the choice is Course Private, what the Project Students share about their Project is Course Private Information, and each must keep it within the Course Community unless all of them agree otherwise. If the choice is Open, nothing is restricted.

**4.6 Protecting Restricted Information.** Each Project Signer who receives Restricted Information must (a) use it only for the Project and the Course, and as Sections 5.5, 6.7, and 6.8 allow; (b) protect it with at least reasonable care; (c) not post or store it in any public forum, such as a public website, code repository, or social media; and (d) share it only as Section 3.2 allows, or as Partner approves in writing.

**4.7 Partner's duties.** Partner must not give anyone in the Course anything listed on the Commerce Control List of the Export Administration Regulations or the U.S. Munitions List of the International Traffic in Arms Regulations. Unless the choice is Open, Partner must label any Protected Data as Course Private Information or Confidential Information.

**4.8 End of the Project.** Before their End Date, and before Course computing resources holding Project Work Product are shut down, each Project Student must make reasonable efforts to ensure that Partner and each teammate receive the copies they are entitled to. Within 30 days after their End Date, or sooner if Partner asks, each Project Signer must (a) delete all Confidential Information, including from personal devices, cloud accounts, and generative AI tool histories, except material licensed under Section 6.8; (b) keep any retained Course Private Information within the Course Community; (c) return or delete Partner's access credentials and stop using its systems; and (d) confirm these steps in writing if Partner asks. A Partner Contact may let a Project Signer keep more, by email.

## 5. Intellectual property

**5.1 The choices.** Each Schedule chooses who owns the Project Work Product: Students own (the default); Students and Partner own jointly, with an FSL license for software; or Partner owns. A team without a Partner may instead choose joint ownership among its Project Students, with an FSL license. Where students own, the Schedule also chooses public licenses. If a choice is blank, has more than one box checked (other than MIT and Apache 2.0, which together mean both), or is not available to the Project, the default applies. All rights under this Section 5 are subject to Harvard's rights (Section 9).

**5.2 Partner Background IP.** "**Partner Background IP**" means the data, software, application programming interfaces, models, and other materials that Partner gives for its Project, and all intellectual property rights in them and in other Partner Information. Partner keeps all rights in it. Project Work Product excludes Partner Background IP, but includes improvements to it and works derived from it, such as a model fine-tuned on Partner data. Partner grants each Project Signer a non-exclusive, non-transferable, royalty-free license to use Partner Background IP during the Duration, only for the Project and while taking part in it. No other right in it is granted, except under Sections 6.7 and 6.8.

**5.3 Third-party materials.** Third-party and open-source materials are not Project Work Product. This Master Agreement does not assign or license them, and their own licenses pass through to recipients.

**5.4 Student Companies.** A "**Student Company**" of a Project Student is a company that the student founded or co-founded, or in which the Project Students together hold at least 25% of the voting equity, in each case when it first uses the Project Work Product. For a Project with a Partner, no competitor of Partner may control it at that time. A Student Company's rights in Project Work Product are never exclusive. What it builds afterwards is its own.

**5.5 Continued use.** Each Project Student may keep using the Project Work Product for demonstrations, in a portfolio, and in the Project's final poster and video, subject to Sections 3, 4, and 6. If Partner owns it, Partner grants each Project Student a non-exclusive, royalty-free, perpetual, worldwide license for those uses.

**5.6 Leaving.** A student's contributions stay under the Schedule in force at their End Date, unless they sign a later change. A student who leaves before the end of the Duration gets no rights under this Section 5 or Section 7, apart from public licenses, in work created after their End Date.

### 5.7 Students own (default)

(a) Each Project Student keeps their rights in the Project Work Product.

(b) Each Project Student licenses those rights to everyone who receives a copy, under the public licenses on the Schedule. For software: the MIT License (default), the Apache License 2.0, both (each recipient chooses), none, or another named license. For everything else, such as documentation, reports, data, and media: CC BY 4.0 (default), CC BY-SA 4.0, none, or another named license. Any release remains subject to Sections 3, 4, and 6.

(c) Each Project Student grants Partner a perpetual, irrevocable, non-exclusive, royalty-free, worldwide license under their rights to use, reproduce, modify, prepare derivative works of, distribute, perform, display, make, have made, sell, offer to sell, import, and otherwise exploit the Project Work Product for any purpose. It transfers only with an assignment under Section 12.3. Partner may not sublicense it without the consent of the Project Students whose rights are involved.

(d) Each Project Student also grants teammates the licenses in Section 7.

(e) Without a Partner, paragraph (c) does not apply. A student on an individual project who chooses no public licenses keeps all rights.

### 5.8 Students and Partner own jointly (FSL)

This choice applies to the software in the Project Work Product (the "**Project Software**"), which is licensed under the Functional Source License, Version 1.1, with the MIT License (FSL-1.1-MIT, the default) or the Apache License 2.0 (FSL-1.1-ALv2) as its future license (the "**FSL license**"). Everything else is treated as under Section 5.7.

(a) Each Project Student assigns to Partner, and to each teammate, an undivided interest in their rights in the Project Software. Partner and the Project Students then own it jointly and are together its licensor under the FSL license.

(b) Each joint owner may use the Project Software for any purpose, personally or through an entity it controls, and Project Students may also use it through a Student Company. No such use is a competing use under the FSL license.

(c) No joint owner may license or distribute the Project Software other than under the FSL license, except to an entity through which paragraph (b) lets it use the software, unless the Schedule is first changed to allow it.

(d) No joint owner owes another a share of profits from a use or license that this Section 5.8 permits.

(e) Each released copy must carry a notice reading "Copyright", followed by the year of first release, Partner's full legal name, and the full names of the Project Students. Project Students may, without the other joint owners' consent, assign their interests to a Student Company and name it in the notice instead, once it agrees in writing to be bound as they are.

(f) Without a Partner, the Project Students own the Project Software jointly, and references to Partner do not apply. A majority of them may, by written notice to all of them, name or replace one Project Student or Student Company as the "**FSL Representative**", to manage releases and enforce the FSL license for all of them. The Schedule may name the first.

> **Note for counsel (review notes R-2 and R-7):** This Section relies on U.S. rules for co-owned works: use and non-exclusive licensing without consent; a duty to account for copyright profits (waived in paragraph (d)); and joinder of all co-owners to enforce. Please confirm, and confirm that the joint licensor can enforce the FSL license.

### 5.9 Partner owns (assignment)

This choice requires a Partner.

(a) Each Project Student assigns to Partner all right, title, and interest in the Project Work Product, including all intellectual property rights, and waives moral rights to the extent the law allows.

(b) At Partner's request and expense, each Project Student must sign documents and take other reasonable steps to perfect, register, or enforce Partner's rights, including patent applications.

(c) No public license applies unless Partner later releases the work itself. The Schedule's license choices and Section 7 do not apply.

(d) Publication review applies automatically (Section 6.4), unless the information choice is Open, in which case Section 6.5 applies instead.

(e) Each Project Student keeps a license for continued use under Section 5.5.

## 6. Publication and demonstrations

**6.1 Sharing within the Course.** Project Students may share their Project's Course Private Information within the Course Community, but never in a public forum without the written consent of Partner and the other Project Signers.

**6.2 Private academic uses.** Even where publication review applies, a Project Student may, without Partner's consent, disclose information about the Project in private coursework, class presentations, Private Course Events, and similar private academic uses, if (a) Partner, and any entity that controls, is controlled by, or is under common control with Partner (a "**Partner Affiliate**"), are masked with generic names, such as "Climate Technology Firm"; (b) Partner receives a copy no later than when the work is submitted or presented; and (c) the student does not unreasonably refuse edits that Partner requests. Confidential Information may still go only to Partner and the Project Signers.

**6.3 Approved Poster and Video.** The "**Approved Poster and Video**" is the final poster or video, or both, that the Project Students submit for the Course, with any edits Partner requests under Section 6.2. Even where publication review applies, a Project Student may show it, and describe its contents, to potential employers, graduate programs, and similar audiences. Without Partner's written consent, they may not publish it in writing or distribute copies or links. If the information choice is Open, they may publish it once Partner's edits are made, subject to Section 6.5, with Partner and any Partner Affiliate still masked unless Partner agrees otherwise in writing.

**6.4 Publication review.** Publication review applies if the Schedule requests it or Partner owns the Project Work Product, unless the information choice is Open. It covers any publication, presentation, or other disclosure relating to Partner Information or, if Partner owns, to the Project Work Product, other than those that Sections 6.2, 6.3, and 6.7 permit. A Project Student must give the Partner Contacts a copy (for an oral disclosure, a summary) at least 30 days before submitting it. The student must not disclose it until Partner consents in writing or the hold period stated on the Schedule (the "**Publication Hold Period**") ends. That period starts when Partner receives the copy; if no period is stated, it lasts until Partner consents. Partner must not unreasonably withhold consent for results that contain no Confidential Information. Any delay for a patent filing is handled within this review and does not extend the Publication Hold Period. Its end ends no duty to protect Restricted Information.

**6.5 Patent notice when Partner owns and information is Open.** Publication review then does not apply. Instead, a Project Student must give Partner written notice, with a copy, at least 30 days before any publication or other public disclosure of Project Work Product, including the Approved Poster and Video. Partner may, by written notice within those 30 days, delay the disclosure until up to 60 days after the student's notice, to file a patent application.

> **Note for counsel:** Patent law may treat a Private Course Event as a public disclosure. Where Partner owns, consider requiring that Partner receive copies under Section 6.2 [10] business days before the event.

**6.6 Credit.** Before Partner or a Project Student submits material about the Project Work Product for publication, Partner's contributors and the other Project Students must be offered co-authorship, unless the submission covers substantial work done after the Duration. A teammate who is not a co-author must be acknowledged, unless they ask not to be. Co-authors must have reasonable time to review a draft and request changes, and teammates to review it and comment. A teammate may be asked to sign a reasonable nondisclosure agreement before seeing a confidential draft. This Section 6.6 does not apply to uses under Sections 6.2 and 6.3. During the Duration, a Partner Contact and all Project Signers may change this process by email.

**6.7 Demonstrations.** Even where publication review applies, a Project Student may demonstrate working Project Work Product live to potential employers, graduate programs, and similar audiences, if they mask Partner and any Partner Affiliate, disclose no Confidential Information, and give no copies of Project Work Product or of Partner's data or code. They may also record demonstrations, redacting Confidential Information, and share them non-publicly (for example, behind a password) with Partner's consent, which Partner must not unreasonably withhold.

**6.8 Enabling License.** Partner grants each Project Student a license, for the Confidentiality Period, to use limited or simulated versions of data or code in Partner Background IP, as minimally needed for noncommercial demonstrations under Sections 5.5 and 6.7 (the "**Enabling License**"). It covers what a Partner Contact identifies in writing. Partner must use commercially reasonable efforts to identify enough for those demonstrations, and the Partner Contacts decide what to identify. For example, a student may demonstrate a model trained on Partner data, but may not extract, reuse, or disclose that data. Licensed material stays restricted as before, and its source code may not be disclosed without Partner's written consent. The Enabling License permits no commercial use, and no commercial use of Project Work Product may rely on it. Job interviews and research discussions are not commercial use.

## 7. Teammates' rights

**7.1 Scope.** This Section 7 applies to every Project with two or more Project Students, with or without a Partner, unless Partner owns the Project Work Product. Teammates may enforce it, and Sections 4.8 and 6.6, against each other directly, so that no one student can deadlock the team.

**7.2 Teammate License.** Each Project Student grants each teammate a license under their rights in the Project Work Product with the same scope as Partner's license in Section 5.7(c), including for products and services sold to customers (a "**Teammate License**"). A Teammate License extends to the teammate's Student Company, but otherwise may not be transferred or sublicensed without the grantor's written consent. No teammate owes another a share of profits from a use that this Master Agreement permits.

**7.3 Separate companies.** Course work stays non-exclusive. Teammates who start separate companies each keep their Teammate Licenses, and each company may use the Project Work Product independently.

**7.4 Buyout ("use it or lose it").**

(a) This Section 7.4 applies only to a category of Project Work Product (software, or everything else) that students own under Section 5.7 with no public license. It does not apply under joint ownership.

(b) A Student Company of one or more Project Students that is actively developing that work may give written notice to a teammate who is not using it commercially, personally or through a company.

(c) The teammate may join the company on the same terms as its other student founders within [60] days after the notice. Otherwise, the teammate must assign their ownership share in that work to the company for the price in paragraph (d). The assignment takes effect when that period ends and the company has paid the price, or has promised in writing to pay it when due. The teammate appoints the company as their attorney-in-fact to sign documents confirming the assignment.

(d) The price is [Alternative 1: the greater of (i) what each founding student received for assigning their own rights in the work to the company, and (ii) [0.01]% of the company's fully diluted equity when the assignment takes effect] [Alternative 2: [percentage]% of the proceeds of any sale of the company or of substantially all its assets, up to $[amount], paid at the sale].

(e) [The sale ends the teammate's Teammate License in that work, but the teammate may still use it for portfolio, academic, and other noncommercial purposes.] [The sale does not end the teammate's Teammate License.]

(f) The buyout does not affect Partner's license, other teammates' rights, or Section 5.5.

> **Note for counsel:** Choose one alternative in paragraph (d), and one option in paragraph (e), before use. Please advise on enforceability (a forced sale in an agreement students sign to take a course; the self-executing assignment and appointment; whether "same terms" is definite enough; and whether to add a time limit), securities law (equity for a non-founder under Alternative 1 or on joining), tax (equity or a deferred payment for intellectual property), and the profit-sharing waiver in Section 7.2.

## 8. Course Staff

**8.1 Personal capacity.** Course Staff sign this Master Agreement, and any Schedule, personally and not for Harvard. Their signatures do not bind Harvard.

**8.2 No intellectual property.** Course Staff advise and evaluate. They must not contribute intellectual property to any Project, and they claim no rights in Project Work Product. Their evaluations, grades, and feedback are not Project Work Product, and Project Students and Partners may use their suggestions freely.

**8.3 Student records.** Course Staff must not share students' grades or other education records, as defined in the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act, 20 U.S.C. § 1232g, with any Partner. No Partner may ask for them, and a Partner that receives any must disregard and promptly delete them. Students may share their own work with a Partner.

**8.4 Confidential Information.** A member of the Course Staff may see a Project's Confidential Information only by signing its Schedule, and is then bound by Section 4 as a Project Signer, but not by Sections 5 and 7. One who does not sign has no duties of a Project Signer for that Project.

## 9. Harvard

**9.1 Not a party.** Harvard is not a party, has no obligations under this Master Agreement, and is not responsible for anyone's performance of it. Its approval of the templates, kept on file, does not make it a party. Harvard makes no representations or warranties about any Project or Project Work Product.

**9.2 Harvard's prior rights.** This Master Agreement does not amend any agreement between Harvard and a Partner or other third party about the Course or a Project (a "**Separate Agreement**"). It does not affect Harvard's rights under a Separate Agreement or under Harvard's policies, including rights from faculty-sponsored research, and it assigns or licenses no right that Harvard owns. Between Harvard and a Partner, a Separate Agreement governs any conflict with this Master Agreement.

**9.3 Coursework exception.** Under a conditional informal exception to its Statement of Policy in Regard to Intellectual Property, Harvard claims no rights in intellectual property that students create in coursework, if the only Harvard-administered resources they use are those generally provided to students in the Course. The exception does not apply if a box under "Harvard's rights" in the Course details is checked; students' rights are then subject to Harvard's claims and any Separate Agreement. Using other Harvard resources, such as some research computing, may also take work outside the exception.

## 11. Changes

**11.1 This Master Agreement is fixed.** Its terms are the same for every party, and no one may amend them for this offering; the next offering will use a new master agreement. A Schedule may set only the choices and blanks on the approved form.

**11.2 Changing a Schedule.** During the Duration, a Project's choices and blanks may change only by a changed Schedule. It must be signed by Partner's authorized officer, if there is a Partner, and by every Project Signer whose End Date has not passed. If the result is not Open Science, Partner (or, without a Partner, the Project Students) must certify on it that a Course instructor approved the change in writing (email is enough). A change takes effect when the last required person signs it. A new information choice applies to information given afterwards. A new ownership choice also covers earlier Project Work Product of those who sign, and replaces the licenses among them for that work, unless the changed Schedule says otherwise. No change withdraws a public license for copies already released. A student who does not accept a change may ask to be reassigned under Section 3.8.

**11.3 No change needed.** Joining, a new list of Partner Contacts, naming an FSL Representative, and the consents, approvals, and notices that this Master Agreement lets Partner, a Partner Contact, or the Project Signers give, need no changed Schedule.

**11.4 After the Duration.** After the Duration, a Project's Partner, through its authorized officer, and all its Project Signers may agree in writing to other terms for their Project, if those terms affect no one else's rights.

## 12. General terms

**12.1 Survival.** Sections 2.5 and 3 to 12 survive each party's End Date and the end of the Duration. Sections 6.2 to 6.5 have no time limit.

**12.2 Partner's officer, Partner Contacts, and notices.** Partner's authorized officer signs its Partner Joinder, its Schedule, any changed Schedule, and any waiver. The Partner Contacts may give Partner's consents, approvals, and notices; give and identify Partner Information; identify material for the Enabling License; request edits; and receive copies. They may not change a Schedule. Partner may change its Partner Contacts by notice to its Project Signers. Notices, copies, consents, and requests must be in writing, and email is enough. Those for Partner go to its Partner Contacts. Others go to the email addresses on the signed documents, or to another address given by notice.

**12.3 Assignment.** Students and Course Staff may not assign this Master Agreement, except to a Student Company under Section 5.8(e) or 7.4. A Partner may assign its rights and duties for its Project, on written notice to its Project Signers, to a successor to all or substantially all of the business to which the Project relates. This Master Agreement binds and benefits permitted successors and assigns.

**12.4 Injunctions.** A breach of Sections 3 to 8 may cause irreparable harm. A Partner, and any other party entitled to enforce the term breached, may seek an injunction in addition to any other remedy.

**12.5 Law and courts.** Massachusetts law governs this Master Agreement, without regard to its conflict-of-laws rules. The state and federal courts in Massachusetts have exclusive jurisdiction over disputes under it, and each party consents to their jurisdiction.

**12.6 Signatures.** This Master Agreement, the Signature Roster, each Partner Joinder, and each Schedule may be signed in counterparts and electronically, including through Adobe Acrobat Sign. Together they form one agreement.

**12.7 Authority.** Each party represents that it may lawfully sign and perform this Master Agreement, that each individual signer has reached the age of majority, and that anyone signing for a Partner is authorized to do so.

**12.8 Interpretation.** "Including" means "including without limitation." In terms about a Project, "Partner" means that Project's Partner. Each party's duties are several, not joint, and no one answers for another's breach. "Reasonable care" and "reasonable efforts" mean what a reasonable person would do in the recipient's position, considering their role in the Course, the information, and when they learned that it was not meant for them.

**12.9 Entire agreement; severability; waiver.** This Master Agreement, with the Signature Roster, the Partner Joinders, and the Schedules, is the parties' entire agreement on its subject. An unenforceable term is enforced as far as the law permits, and the rest stays in effect. A waiver must be in writing, and delay is not a waiver.

**12.10 Who may enforce.** A party may enforce a term only if it protects that party. For example, a Project's Partner and Project Signers may enforce its terms against each other, and Partners and Project Students may enforce Section 3 as Section 3.1 states. Harvard, though not a party, may enforce Sections 9 and 10.5 as a third-party beneficiary. No one else has rights under this Master Agreement.

**12.11 Records.** The Course Staff keep the signed documents. Each party may have a copy of this Master Agreement and of each document it signed. Partners do not receive the Signature Roster or other Projects' documents.
