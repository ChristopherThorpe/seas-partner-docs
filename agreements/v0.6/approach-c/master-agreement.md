## 1. How this agreement works

**1.1 Parties.** This course project master agreement (this "**Master Agreement**") governs projects in the course offering named above (the "**Course**") at the Harvard John A. Paulson School of Engineering and Applied Sciences ("**SEAS**"). The "**Duration**" is every term and session of that offering. The parties are:

- each student in the Course who signs the signature roster at the end (the "**Signature Roster**");
- each instructor, teaching assistant, teaching fellow, course assistant, or other member of the Course's teaching staff (together, the "**Course Staff**") who signs the Signature Roster; and
- each organization that signs a project's schedule as its partner (that project's "**Partner**").

President and Fellows of Harvard College ("**Harvard**") is not a party.

**1.2 Projects.** Each project in the Course (a "**Project**") has one project schedule in the approved form (a "**Schedule**"), which records its choices and blanks. A Project has one Partner or none. The students who sign a Project's Schedule are its "**Project Students**", and each is a "**teammate**" of the others. The Project Students and any Course Staff who sign the Schedule are its "**Project Signers**". "**Project Work Product**" means all results, reports, presentations, posters, videos, software, data, designs, and other work product that any Project Student creates, alone or with others, in the course of the Project.

**1.3 One agreement, separate Projects.** Each party agrees with every other party, including those who sign later, in exchange for their promises. A Project's terms bind and benefit only its Partner and Project Signers, except where this Master Agreement says otherwise.

**1.4 Partners.** A Partner is a party only for its own Project. Its rights and duties are those that this Master Agreement gives a Partner for its Project, plus Sections 3, 4.3, 9, 10, and 12 as they apply to Partners. Partners owe each other nothing else, and no Partner has any right in another Project. A Partner that sponsors two Projects signs each Project's Schedule separately. A Partner acts through its authorized officers, and through the individuals its Schedule names as its "**Partner Contacts**".

> **Note for counsel:** A partner's lawyer will likely ask whether joining an agreement with other companies, possibly competitors, creates duties or liability toward them (Sections 1.4 and 10.4 aim to prevent this), raises antitrust concerns, or binds the partner to later signers without further action (Section 1.3). A partner's compliance team may also want to screen counterparties that it cannot see (Section 12.11). Please confirm each point.

**1.5 Coursework.** Each Project is an educational activity of the Course, graded solely by the Course Staff, and a Partner may benefit from its results. No Partner employs or pays any student or member of the Course Staff. This Master Agreement creates no agency, partnership, or fiduciary relationship, and no one must offer or accept employment.

## 2. Choosing, joining, and leaving a Project

**2.1 Open Science is the default.** An "**Open Science**" project is one whose Schedule has, by choice or by default: Open information; Students own; the MIT License, the Apache License 2.0, or both, for software; and CC BY 4.0 or CC BY-SA 4.0 for everything else. If a choice on a Schedule is blank, has more than one box checked (other than MIT and Apache 2.0, which together mean both), or is not available to the Project, its default applies. Course Private and Confidential without a Partner means Course Private.

**2.2 Instructor approval.** Any other choice needs a Course instructor's approval in writing (email is enough), at signing and for any later change. An individual project (one Project Student) always needs it, with or without a Partner. Partner certifies that approval on the Schedule; without a Partner, the Project Students do. The instructor does not sign. If a choice that needs approval has no approval recorded on the Schedule, its default applies.

**2.3 Before starting.** Before choosing a Project, each student must read the Student Guide to Course Project Agreements (the "**Student Guide**") and confirm on the Signature Roster that they have. The Student Guide explains this Master Agreement but binds no one. Each student must sign a Project's Schedule before receiving its Partner's information or starting material work on it.

**2.4 Joining.** Each person is bound from the date they sign, and a Schedule also covers information given to its Project before then. A Schedule that names a Partner binds no one until that Partner signs it. A student who joins the Course late signs the Signature Roster then. Anyone who joins a Project later signs the joinder block on its Schedule; joining is not a change. A Project's choices apply to all its Project Students alike.

**2.5 End Date and Confidentiality Period.** A person's "**End Date**" for a Project is the earlier of the end of the Duration and the date they stop taking part in it, including by withdrawing from the Course. For duties under Section 3, a person's End Date is the earlier of the end of the Duration and the date they leave the Course. A Partner's End Date is always the end of the Duration. Every duty under this Master Agreement to protect information lasts until five years after the person's End Date (the "**Confidentiality Period**"), and for a trade secret, as long as it remains one.

**2.6 Capstone.** If the Course is a capstone for a student's degree, the student may decline every partner Project and do a Project without a Partner. The student was also offered the choice of writing a thesis instead.

## 3. Rules for everyone in the Course

**3.1 Scope.** This Section 3 binds every student and member of the Course Staff, whether or not they sign a Schedule, and protects every Project. Each Partner, and each Project's Project Students, may enforce this Section 3 for their own Project's information.

**3.2 Definitions.**

- A "**Private Course Event**" is a class session, presentation, poster session, video showcase, project review, or similar Course event that is not open to the public. It is limited to [the students, faculty, and staff of SEAS (the "**SEAS community**")] and invited guests.
- The "**Course Community**" is (a) students enrolled in the Course during the Duration; (b) the Course Staff; (c) Harvard faculty and staff who support the Course; and (d) [the SEAS community] and invited guests attending a Private Course Event, while attending it.
- "**Partner Information**" is all information, in any form, that a Partner, or anyone acting for it, gives or makes available for its Project.
- "**Course Private Information**" may be shared only with its Partner and within the Course Community. It is Partner Information when its Project's Schedule so provides, and a Project's own information when a Project without a Partner so chooses.
- "**Confidential Information**" is Partner Information that its Partner marks under Section 5.3. It may be shared only with its Partner and its Project's Project Signers.
- Course Private Information and Confidential Information are together "**Restricted Information**".

> **Note for counsel:** (Review note R-8.) Please settle who counts as the SEAS community (for example, cross-registered and Extension School students, postdocs, visiting scholars, other Harvard schools, and alumni) and how membership is shown. Please also consider whether the Course Staff should tell attendees at each Private Course Event not to photograph, record, or share Course Private Information. As in v0.5, invited guests, including Partners' representatives, owe no duty to keep other Projects' Course Private Information private.

**3.3 Inadvertent Disclosures.** An "**Inadvertent Disclosure**" occurs when anyone's confidential information reaches anyone, in connection with the Course, who is not authorized to receive it, for example through a mistaken file or a sharing error. Any student, member of the Course Staff, or Partner who receives one must use reasonable care to (a) tell the sender, or the Course Staff if the sender is unknown; (b) not copy, forward, or otherwise use it; and (c) delete every copy, including notes and photos. Receipt alone creates no liability, but intentional use or disclosure afterwards does. Taking steps (a) to (c) promptly is reasonable care. Course Private Information shared within the Course Community is not an Inadvertent Disclosure.

**3.4 Other Projects' Course Private Information.** Each student and member of the Course Staff must keep the Course Private Information of any Project whose Schedule they have not signed within the Course Community, and use it only for the Course. They must never pass it to any Partner other than that Project's own. This applies to information that they know, or have reason to know, is Course Private Information. That Project's Partner and Project Students (or, without a Partner, its Project Students) may consent otherwise in writing. No Partner may ask anyone for another Project's Restricted Information without that consent.

**3.5 Others' confidential information.** No student or member of the Course Staff may share with any Partner or anyone else in the Course, or use in a Project, anyone else's confidential information that they are not authorized to share or use, such as a current or former employer's.

**3.6 Generative AI.** Students must use their Harvard accounts for Course work and, if they use generative AI tools, Harvard-provided ones, unless the Course Staff allow otherwise or a Partner asks its Project to use the Partner's tools at its expense. A Partner's Restricted Information may be used only with generative AI tools that the Partner approves in writing (email is enough), which may include Harvard's.

**3.7 Exclusions and permitted disclosures.** No duty to protect information applies to information that the recipient can show is or becomes public through no breach, was known to them without restriction before receipt, came from a third party with no duty of confidentiality, or was independently developed without use of it. Anyone may disclose information as a law, regulation, subpoena, or court order requires, after giving its owner prompt notice where permitted, and reasonable help at the owner's expense to seek a protective order. Nothing prevents reporting a possible violation of law to a government agency, or any other legally protected disclosure. Under 18 U.S.C. § 1833(b), an individual will not be held criminally or civilly liable under any federal or state trade secret law for disclosing a trade secret in confidence to a government official or an attorney, solely to report or investigate a suspected violation of law. The same applies to a disclosure in a court filing made under seal.

**3.8 Reassignment.** A student may ask to be assigned to an Open Science project or a Project without a Partner. A student may also ask to move to another Project, including either kind, and the Course instructors will honor that request. Work the student did on the first Project stays under its terms as they stood when the student left it, as if the student had signed its Schedule, and the student assigns or licenses that work as those terms provide. The student must keep the first Partner's information confidential as Course Private Information, or more strictly if that Project's terms require. That Project's Partner and Project Signers may enforce this Section 3.8.

## 4. Course Staff

**4.1 Personal capacity.** Course Staff sign this Master Agreement, and any Schedule, personally and not for Harvard.

**4.2 No intellectual property.** Course Staff advise and evaluate. They must not contribute intellectual property to any Project. Their evaluations, grades, and feedback are not Project Work Product. If a member of the Course Staff contributes anyway, they grant the Project Students and Partner a perpetual, irrevocable, non-exclusive, royalty-free license, so far as they can, to use that contribution for any purpose, and they claim no other rights in it.

> **Note for counsel:** Course Staff are Harvard employees, so Harvard may own their work, and a member of the Course Staff cannot license or waive Harvard's rights. Should Harvard's approval license or disclaim such contributions for the listed courses?

**4.3 Student records.** Course Staff must not share students' grades or other education records, as defined in the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act, 20 U.S.C. § 1232g, with any Partner, except as a student consents on the Signature Roster. No Partner may ask for them, and a Partner that receives any must disregard and promptly delete them. Students may share their own work with a Partner.

**4.4 Confidential Information.** A member of the Course Staff may see a Project's Confidential Information only by signing its Schedule, and is then bound by Section 5 as a Project Signer, but not by Sections 6 and 8.

## 5. Information choices

**5.1 Open (default).** Partner must not give any information that it needs kept confidential, or any personal data, third-party data, or other data restricted by law or contract ("**Protected Data**"). Nothing about the Project is confidential except as Section 3 requires.

**5.2 Course Private.** All Partner Information is Course Private Information. If Partner gives something by mistake and identifies it as confidential, each Project Signer must, when Partner asks in writing, delete it, stop using it, and make reasonable efforts to remove it from submitted coursework. Earlier uses that were permitted for Course Private Information are not a breach.

**5.3 Course Private and Confidential.** This choice needs a Partner. All Partner Information is Course Private Information unless Partner clearly marks it as Confidential Information: in writing when it is given or, for an oral or visual disclosure, at the time, with written confirmation within ten business days. The rule in Section 5.2 for something given by mistake also applies. Partner may give Confidential Information only to Project Signers.

**5.4 Without a Partner.** If the choice is Course Private, what the Project Students share about their Project is Course Private Information, and each must keep it within the Course Community unless all of them agree otherwise. If the choice is Open, nothing is restricted.

**5.5 Protecting Restricted Information.** Each Project Signer who receives Restricted Information must (a) use it only for the Project and the Course, and as Sections 6.5, 7.7, and 7.8 allow; (b) protect it with at least reasonable care; (c) not post or store it in any public forum, such as a public website, code repository, or social media; and (d) share it only as Section 3.2 allows, or as Partner approves in writing.

**5.6 Partner's duties.** Partner must not give anyone in the Course anything listed on the Commerce Control List of the Export Administration Regulations or the U.S. Munitions List of the International Traffic in Arms Regulations. Unless the choice is Open, Partner must label any Protected Data as Course Private Information or Confidential Information.

**5.7 End of the Project.** Before their End Date, and before Course computing resources holding Project Work Product are shut down, each Project Student must make reasonable efforts to ensure that Partner and each teammate receive the copies they are entitled to. Within 30 days after their End Date, or sooner if Partner asks, each Project Signer must (a) delete all Confidential Information, including from personal devices, cloud accounts, and generative AI tool histories, except material licensed under Section 7.8; (b) keep any retained Course Private Information within the Course Community; (c) return or delete Partner's access credentials and stop using its systems; and (d) confirm these steps in writing if Partner asks. A Partner Contact may let a Project Signer keep more, by email.

## 6. Intellectual property

**6.1 The choices.** Each Schedule chooses who owns the Project Work Product: Students own (the default); Students and Partner own jointly; or Partner owns. A team without a Partner may instead choose joint ownership among its Project Students. Joint ownership requires the Functional Source License, Version 1.1, for software, with the MIT License (FSL-1.1-MIT, the default) or the Apache License 2.0 (FSL-1.1-ALv2) as its future license (the "**FSL license**"). Where students own, the Schedule also chooses public licenses. All rights under this Section 6 are subject to Harvard's rights (Section 9).

**6.2 Partner Background IP.** "**Partner Background IP**" means the data, software, models, and other materials that Partner gives for its Project, and all intellectual property rights in them and in other Partner Information. Partner keeps all rights in it. Project Work Product excludes Partner Background IP, but includes improvements to it and works derived from it, such as a model fine-tuned on Partner data. Partner grants each Project Signer a non-exclusive, non-transferable, royalty-free license to use Partner Background IP during the Duration, only for the Project and while taking part in it. No other right in it is granted, except under Sections 7.7 and 7.8.

**6.3 Third-party materials.** Third-party and open-source materials are not Project Work Product. This Master Agreement does not assign or license them, and their own licenses pass through to recipients.

**6.4 Student Companies.** A "**Student Company**" of a Project Student is a company that the student founded or co-founded, or in which the Project Students together hold at least 25% of the voting equity. Both tests apply when the company first uses the Project Work Product or receives an assignment under Section 6.8(e) or 8.3. For a Project with a Partner, no competitor of Partner may control it at that time. Apart from a buyout under Section 8.3, a Student Company's rights in Project Work Product are non-exclusive, and what it builds afterwards is its own.

**6.5 Continued use.** Each Project Student may keep using the Project Work Product for demonstrations, in a portfolio, and in the Project's final poster and video, subject to Sections 3, 5, and 7. If Partner owns it, Partner grants each Project Student a non-exclusive, royalty-free, perpetual, worldwide license for those uses.

**6.6 Leaving.** A student's contributions stay under the Schedule in force at their End Date, unless they sign a later change. A student who leaves before the end of the Duration gets no rights under this Section 6 or Section 8, apart from public licenses, in work created after their End Date.

### 6.7 Students own (default)

(a) Each Project Student keeps their rights in the Project Work Product.

(b) Each Project Student licenses those rights to everyone who receives a copy, under the public licenses chosen on the Schedule: by default, the MIT License for software and CC BY 4.0 for everything else. If MIT and Apache 2.0 are both chosen, each recipient chooses. Any release remains subject to Sections 3, 5, and 7.

(c) Each Project Student grants Partner a perpetual, irrevocable, non-exclusive, royalty-free, worldwide license under their rights to use, reproduce, modify, prepare derivative works of, distribute, perform, display, make, have made, sell, offer to sell, import, and otherwise exploit the Project Work Product for any purpose. It transfers only with an assignment under Section 12.3. Partner may not sublicense it without the consent of the Project Students whose rights are involved. Providing the Project Work Product, or products or services that include it, to customers and users on ordinary end-user terms is not sublicensing.

(d) Without a Partner, a student on an individual project who chooses no public licenses keeps all rights.

### 6.8 Students and Partner own jointly (FSL)

This choice covers the software in the Project Work Product (the "**Project Software**"), which is licensed under the FSL license chosen on the Schedule. Everything else is treated as under Section 6.7.

(a) Each Project Student assigns to Partner, and to each teammate[, including one who joins later], an [equal] undivided interest in their rights in the Project Software. Partner and the Project Students then own it jointly and are together its licensor under the FSL license.

(b) Each joint owner may use the Project Software for any purpose, personally or through an entity it controls, and Project Students may also use it through a Student Company. No such use is a competing use under the FSL license.

(c) No joint owner may license or distribute the Project Software other than under the FSL license, except to an entity through which paragraph (b) lets it use the software, unless the Schedule is first changed to allow it.

(d) No joint owner owes another a share of profits from a use or license that this Section 6.8 permits.

(e) Each released copy must carry a notice reading "Copyright", the year of first release, Partner's full legal name, and the full names of the Project Students. Project Students may, without the other joint owners' consent, assign their interests to a Student Company and name it in the notice instead, once it agrees in writing to be bound as they are.

(f) Without a Partner, the Project Students own the Project Software jointly. A majority of them may, by written notice to all of them, name or replace one Project Student or Student Company as the "**FSL Representative**", to manage releases and enforce the FSL license for all of them. The Schedule may name the first.

> **Note for counsel:** (Review notes R-2 and R-7.) This Section relies on U.S. rules for co-owned works: use and non-exclusive licensing without consent; a duty to account for copyright profits (waived in paragraph (d)); and joinder of all co-owners to enforce. Please confirm, and confirm that the joint licensor can enforce the FSL license. Please also choose whether the interests are equal, and whether a student who joins later takes an interest in earlier code (the brackets in paragraph (a)).

### 6.9 Partner owns (assignment)

This choice requires a Partner.

(a) Each Project Student assigns to Partner all right, title, and interest in the Project Work Product, including all intellectual property rights, and waives moral rights to the extent the law allows.

(b) At Partner's request and expense, each Project Student must sign documents and take other reasonable steps to perfect, register, or enforce Partner's rights, including patent applications.

(c) No public license applies unless Partner later releases the work itself. The Schedule's license choices and Section 8 do not apply.

(d) Publication review or the patent notice applies (Sections 7.4 and 7.5), and each Project Student keeps a license for continued use (Section 6.5).

## 7. Publication and demonstrations

**7.1 Sharing within the Course.** Project Students may share their Project's Course Private Information within the Course Community, but never in a public forum without the written consent of Partner and the other Project Signers.

**7.2 Private academic uses.** A Project Student may, without Partner's consent, disclose information about the Project in private coursework, class presentations, Private Course Events, and similar private academic uses. The student must (a) mask Partner, and any entity that controls, is controlled by, or is under common control with Partner (a "**Partner Affiliate**"), with generic names, such as "Climate Technology Firm"; (b) give Partner a copy no later than when the work is submitted or presented; and (c) not unreasonably refuse edits that Partner requests.

**7.3 Approved Poster and Video.** The "**Approved Poster and Video**" is the final poster or video, or both, that the Project Students submit for the Course, with any edits Partner requests under Section 7.2. A Project Student may show it, and describe its contents, to potential employers, graduate programs, and similar audiences. Without Partner's written consent, they may not publish it in writing or distribute copies or links. If the information choice is Open, they may publish it once Partner's edits are made, subject to Section 7.5, with Partner and any Partner Affiliate still masked unless Partner agrees otherwise in writing.

**7.4 Publication review.** Publication review applies if the Schedule requests it or Partner owns the Project Work Product, unless the information choice is Open. It covers any publication, presentation, or other disclosure relating to Partner Information or, if Partner owns, to the Project Work Product, other than one that Section 7.2, 7.3, or 7.7 permits. A Project Student must give the Partner Contacts a copy (for an oral disclosure, a summary) at least 30 days before submitting it. The student must not disclose it until Partner consents in writing or the hold period stated on the Schedule (the "**Publication Hold Period**") ends. That period starts when Partner receives the copy; if no period is stated, it lasts until Partner consents. Partner must not unreasonably withhold consent for results that contain no Confidential Information. Any delay for a patent filing is handled within this review and does not extend the Publication Hold Period. The end of the Publication Hold Period does not end any duty to protect Restricted Information.

**7.5 Patent notice when Partner owns and information is Open.** A Project Student must give Partner written notice, with a copy, at least 30 days before any publication or other public disclosure of Project Work Product, including the Approved Poster and Video. Partner may, by written notice within those 30 days, delay the disclosure until up to 60 days after the student's notice, to file a patent application.

> **Note for counsel:** Patent law may treat a Private Course Event as a public disclosure. Where Partner owns, consider requiring that Partner receive copies under Section 7.2 [10] business days before the event.

**7.6 Credit.** Before Partner or a Project Student submits material about the Project Work Product for publication, Partner's contributors and the other Project Students must be offered co-authorship, unless the submission covers substantial work done after the Duration. A teammate who is not a co-author must be acknowledged, unless they ask not to be. Co-authors must have reasonable time to review a draft and request changes, and teammates to review it and comment. A teammate may be asked to sign a reasonable nondisclosure agreement before seeing a confidential draft. This Section 7.6 does not apply to uses under Sections 7.2 and 7.3. During the Duration, a Partner Contact and all Project Signers may change this process by email.

**7.7 Demonstrations.** A Project Student may demonstrate working Project Work Product live to potential employers, graduate programs, and similar audiences, if they mask Partner and any Partner Affiliate, disclose no Confidential Information, and give no copies of Project Work Product or of Partner's data or code. They may also record demonstrations, redacting Confidential Information, and share them non-publicly (for example, behind a password) with Partner's consent, which Partner must not unreasonably withhold.

**7.8 Enabling License.** Partner grants each Project Student a license, for the Confidentiality Period, to use limited or simulated versions of data or code in Partner Background IP, as minimally needed for noncommercial demonstrations under Sections 6.5 and 7.7 (the "**Enabling License**"). It covers what a Partner Contact identifies in writing. Partner must use commercially reasonable efforts to identify enough, and the Partner Contacts decide what to identify. For example, a student may demonstrate a model trained on Partner data, but may not extract, reuse, or disclose that data. Licensed material stays restricted as before, and its source code may not be disclosed without Partner's written consent. The Enabling License permits no commercial use, and no commercial use of Project Work Product may rely on it. Job interviews and research discussions are not commercial use.

## 8. Teammates' rights

**8.1 Scope.** This Section 8 applies to every Project with two or more Project Students, with or without a Partner, unless Partner owns the Project Work Product. Teammates may enforce it, and Sections 5.7, 6, and 7.6, against each other directly, so that no one student can deadlock the team.

**8.2 Teammate License.** Each Project Student grants each teammate a license under their rights in the Project Work Product with the same scope as Partner's license in Section 6.7(c), whether or not there is a Partner (a "**Teammate License**"). A Teammate License extends to the teammate's Student Company, but otherwise may not be transferred or sublicensed without the grantor's written consent. Under joint ownership, a Teammate License in the Project Software is subject to Section 6.8(c). No teammate owes another a share of profits from a use that this Master Agreement permits. Teammates who start separate companies each keep their Teammate Licenses, and each company may use the Project Work Product independently.

**8.3 Buyout ("use it or lose it").**

(a) This Section 8.3 applies only to a category of Project Work Product (software, or everything else) that students own under Section 6.7 with no public license. It does not apply under joint ownership.

(b) A Student Company of one or more Project Students that is actively developing that work may give written notice to a teammate who is not using it commercially, personally or through a company. The company must send the notice to the teammate's harvard.edu address and to their last known personal email address. If two companies give notice, the first notice governs.

(c) The teammate may join the company on the same terms as its other student founders within [60] days. That period starts when the teammate receives the notice, or [30] days after it is sent, if that is earlier.

(d) If the teammate does not join, the teammate hereby assigns to the company all of their right, title, and interest in that work as an owner, including intellectual property rights. The assignment takes effect when that period ends and the company has paid or tendered the price in paragraph (e)[, or, under Alternative 2, has given the teammate a signed promise to pay it when due]. It takes effect only if the company has first agreed in writing to be bound by this Master Agreement as the teammate was. If the teammate has not signed a document confirming the assignment within 30 days after the company's written request, the teammate appoints the company as their attorney-in-fact to sign, deliver, and record it, including for patents.

(e) The price is [Alternative 1: the greater of (i) the [lowest] [average] value that any founding student received, in cash or equity, for assigning their own rights in the work to the company, and (ii) [0.01]% of the company's fully diluted equity when the assignment takes effect] [Alternative 2: [percentage]% of the proceeds of any sale of the company or of substantially all its assets, up to $[amount], paid at the sale]. If the price includes equity and the teammate does not accept it within 30 days after the company's written offer, the company may pay its fair market value in cash instead.

(f) [The sale ends the teammate's Teammate License in that work, but the teammate may still use it for portfolio, academic, and other noncommercial purposes.] [The sale does not end the teammate's Teammate License.]

(g) The buyout does not affect Partner's license, other teammates' rights, or Section 6.5.

> **Note for counsel:** Choose one alternative in paragraph (e), the bracketed options in paragraphs (d) and (e), and one option in paragraph (f), before use. Please advise on:
>
> - transfer formalities: whether a present assignment in an agreement signed before the work exists, taking effect on a later condition, meets 17 U.S.C. § 204(a) and 35 U.S.C. § 261, and whether the appointment in paragraph (d) is irrevocable as a power coupled with an interest;
> - adhesion and unconscionability: a forced sale in an agreement that students must sign to take a course; whether "the same terms as its other student founders" is definite enough; whether to add a time limit; and whether a promise to pay is acceptable, or the interest should return to the teammate if the company does not pay;

> - tax: equity or a deferred payment received for intellectual property, its valuation, and whether 26 U.S.C. § 1221(a)(3) makes the price ordinary income for a student who wrote the code; and
> - the profit-sharing waiver in Section 8.2: whether it is effective, whether it binds an heir or creditor of a student's interest (Section 12.3 reaches only voluntary transfers), and whether co-ownership rules outside the U.S. respect it.

## 9. Harvard

**9.1 Not a party.** Harvard is not a party, has no obligations under this Master Agreement, and is not responsible for anyone's performance of it. Harvard makes no representations or warranties about any Project or Project Work Product.

**9.2 Harvard's prior rights.** This Master Agreement does not amend any agreement between Harvard and a Partner or other third party about the Course or a Project (a "**Separate Agreement**"). It does not affect Harvard's rights under a Separate Agreement or under Harvard's policies, including rights from faculty-sponsored research, and it assigns or licenses no right that Harvard owns. The exception does not apply if a box under "Harvard's rights" in the Course details is checked; students' rights are then subject to Harvard's claims and any Separate Agreement.

## 11. Changes

**11.1 This Master Agreement is fixed.** Its terms are the same for every party, and no one may amend them for this offering; the next offering will use a new master agreement. A Schedule may set only the choices and blanks on the approved form.

**11.2 Changing a Schedule.** During the Duration, a Project's choices and blanks, apart from its Partner Contacts and FSL Representative, may change only by a changed Schedule. It must be signed by an authorized officer of Partner, if there is a Partner, and by every Project Signer whose End Date has not passed. It must carry any certification that Section 2.2 requires. A change takes effect when the last required person signs it. A new information choice applies to information given afterwards. A new ownership choice also covers earlier Project Work Product of those who sign, and replaces the licenses among them for that work, unless the changed Schedule says otherwise. No change withdraws a public license already granted to anyone who received a copy.

**11.3 After the Duration.** After the Duration, a Project's Partner, through an authorized officer, and all its Project Signers may agree in writing to other terms for their Project, if those terms affect no one else's rights. A change that leaves the Project other than Open Science still needs a Course instructor's approval, certified as Section 2.2 requires.

## 12. General terms

**12.1 Survival.** Sections 1, 2.5, and 3 to 12 survive each party's End Date and the end of the Duration. Sections 7.2 to 7.5 have no time limit.

**12.2 Partner's officers, Partner Contacts, and notices.** An authorized officer of Partner signs its Schedule, any changed Schedule, and any waiver. The Partner Contacts may give Partner's consents, approvals, and notices; give and identify Partner Information; identify material for the Enabling License; request edits; and receive copies and requests for consent. They may not change a Schedule's choices. Partner may change its Partner Contacts by notice to its Project Signers. Notices, copies, consents, and requests must be in writing, and email is enough. Those for Partner go to its Partner Contacts. Others go to the email addresses on the signed documents, including any personal email address on the Signature Roster, or to another address given by notice.

**12.3 Assignment and transfers.** Students and Course Staff may not assign this Master Agreement. A Project Student may transfer an ownership interest in Project Work Product, including to a Student Company under Section 6.8(e) or 8.3, only to someone who first agrees in writing to be bound by this Master Agreement, as it applies to that work, as that student is. A Partner may assign its rights and duties for its Project, on written notice to its Project Signers, to a successor to all or substantially all of the business to which the Project relates. An assignee becomes the Partner for that Project only. This Master Agreement binds and benefits permitted successors and assigns.

**12.4 Injunctions.** A breach of Sections 3 to 8 may cause irreparable harm. A Partner, and any other party entitled to enforce the term breached, may seek an injunction in addition to any other remedy.

**12.5 Law and courts.** Massachusetts law governs this Master Agreement, without regard to its conflict-of-laws rules. The state and federal courts in Massachusetts have exclusive jurisdiction over disputes under it, and each party consents to their jurisdiction.

**12.6 Signatures.** This Master Agreement, the Signature Roster, and each Schedule may be signed in counterparts and electronically, including through Adobe Acrobat Sign.

**12.7 Authority.** Each party represents that it may lawfully sign and perform this Master Agreement. Each individual party represents that they have reached the age of majority. Each individual who signs for a Partner represents that they are authorized to do so.

**12.8 Interpretation.** "Including" means "including without limitation." In terms about a Project, "Partner" means that Project's Partner. For a Project without a Partner, a term that requires Partner's consent or gives Partner a right does not apply, and a term that requires the consent of Partner and others requires only the others' consent. Each party's duties are several, not joint, and no one answers for another's breach. "Reasonable care" and "reasonable efforts" mean what a reasonable person would do in the recipient's position, considering their role in the Course, the information, and when they learned that it was not meant for them.

**12.9 Course policy; entire agreement; severability; waiver.** This Master Agreement is the Course's policy on intellectual property and confidentiality for projects, and it governs if another Course document conflicts with it. This Master Agreement, the Signature Roster, and, for each Project, its Schedule are the parties' entire agreement on their subject. A Partner is bound by no other Project's Schedule. An unenforceable term is enforced as far as the law permits, and the rest stays in effect. A waiver must be in writing, and delay is not a waiver.

**12.10 Who may enforce.** A party may enforce a term only if it protects that party. A Student Company may enforce Sections 6.8, 8.2, and 8.3 as far as they benefit it. Recipients of a public license have the rights that license gives. Harvard, though not a party, may enforce Sections 9 and 10.5 as a third-party beneficiary. No one else has rights under this Master Agreement.

**12.11 Records.** The Course Staff keep the signed documents. Each party may have a copy of this Master Agreement and of each document it signed. Partners do not receive the Signature Roster or other Projects' Schedules. On a Partner's written request about a suspected breach of Section 3, the Course instructors will give it a copy of a named student's signed Roster entry, as that student consented on the Roster.
