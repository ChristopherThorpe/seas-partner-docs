# Approach A: Course Agreement and Project Agreement

## Summary

Approach A splits the agreements by audience. Everyone in a course offering, every student and every member of the Course Staff, signs one **Course Agreement**, and its signers agree with each other. It states once every rule that protects other projects (inadvertent disclosures, other projects' Course Private Information, others' confidential information, and generative AI), the Course Staff's role, teammates' rights (including the bracketed buyout), reassignment, Harvard's position, and the Confidentiality Period. It ends with a one-page **Project Election (No Partner)** for projects without a partner. Each project with a partner then has a **Project Agreement**: a cover page holding every choice and blank, with defaults printed beside each choice, followed by six sections of standard terms that are the same for every project. Partner and every student on the Project are parties to each other, and the Project Agreement refers to the Course Agreement rather than repeating course-wide rules. Harvard approves both templates once, for the listed courses, in a **Harvard approval** kept on file.

## Who signs what, and when

| Stakeholder | Signs | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Every student | Course Agreement (signature roster), confirming they have read the Student Guide | Once per course offering, before project work begins, or on enrolling later |
| Student on a project with a partner | That project's Project Agreement (cover page) | Before receiving partner information or starting material work; a later joiner signs the joinder page |
| Students on a project without a partner | One Project Election (No Partner), at the end of the Course Agreement | Before starting material work; a later joiner signs it on joining |
| Course Staff (instructors, teaching fellows, teaching assistants, and course assistants) | Course Agreement (signature roster), in a personal capacity | Once per course offering, before project work begins, or on joining |
| Course Staff who need a project's Confidential Information | That project's Project Agreement (cover page or joinder page) | Before receiving Confidential Information |
| Course instructor | Nothing. Approves any choice other than Open Science, and any individual project, in writing or by email | At signing and before any later change |
| Partner (authorized officer) | Project Agreement; any change to it | Before project work begins; on any change |
| Partner Contacts | Nothing. Act for Partner by email (consents, generative AI tools, Enabling License, edits); one may acknowledge joinders | During the Project |
| Student Company | A written agreement to be bound, before receiving students' FSL interests | Before the assignment (Project Agreement Section 4.4(f); Course Agreement Section 6.4(e)) |
| Harvard (through OTD, SEAS, or OGC) | Harvard approval | Once per template version, for the listed courses; kept on file, never attached to agreements |

## Length

Approximate word counts. "Text" excludes notes for counsel, which are removed before use, and blank lines in forms.

| Document | Text | Notes for counsel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Course Agreement: Sections 1 to 8 | 3,330 | 180 |
| Course Agreement: signature roster and Project Election | 330 | none |
| Project Agreement: cover page and joinder page | 800 | none |
| Project Agreement: standard terms | 3,970 | 160 |
| Harvard approval | 470 | 20 |

The Course Agreement, with its forms, runs about 3,650 words, above the 2,000 to 3,000 aimed for. The excess is the teammates' rights and buyout (about 750 words) and the no-partner terms (about 450), both of which the brief places in the Course Agreement. Cutting further would mean dropping a decision. The standard terms are within the 3,000 to 4,000 aimed for.

## Strengths

- **Each course-wide rule is stated once.** Every student and member of the Course Staff signs it and can enforce it directly. The v0.5 third-party beneficiary list for students, Exhibit C box 1, and Exhibit D's references to about twenty sections all disappear.
- **The partner's document is short and predictable.** One cover page holds every choice, with defaults, and each IP choice is one complete subsection (PA 4.3 to 4.5).
- **Teammates' rights are the same for every project,** with or without a partner, in one place (CA Section 5).
- **Projects without a partner** need only a one-page form; their terms sit next to the rules they depend on.
- **Harvard signs once,** and the informal exception and Harvard's prior rights are stated where students read them (CA 7.2 and 7.3).
- **The cover page suits the document generator:** every choice is a labeled check box with its default printed beside it.

## Weaknesses

- **Two signatures per student per project.** A student who is assigned a project without having signed the Course Agreement is bound only by the PA. The Course Staff need a process check that the roster is complete before assigning projects.
- **The PA is not self-contained for a partner.** Partner agrees to four Course Agreement sections (3.1, 7.2, 7.4, and 8.2) and its definitions, so the Course Agreement must be given to partners with the PA.
- **Partners still rely on third-party beneficiary rights** to enforce the Course Agreement's rules against students on other projects (CA 8.3).
- **A large multi-party agreement** signed at different times raises accession questions (counsel note at CA 1.1). Changing the Course Agreement mid-offering needs every signer (CA 8.4), so in practice fixes wait for the next offering.
- **The Course Agreement is above its target length** (see "Length").
- **The cover page uses terms defined in the standard terms** (for example, Confidential Information and Partner Information), as cover-page agreements usually do.

## Open questions

The notes for counsel in the documents ask:

1. Whether later Course Agreement signers are bound to, and may enforce against, earlier ones (CA 1.1).
2. Who counts as the SEAS community, and whether attendees must be told (CA 1.3; review note R-8).
3. Whether a Course Staff member's fallback grant works, given that Harvard may own employees' work (CA 4.1).
4. The buyout: which price alternative; whether to keep paragraph (f); enforceability, including the self-executing assignment; and securities and tax treatment (CA 5.5).
5. The gross negligence and willful misconduct carve-outs for Harvard and the Course Staff (CA 7.4; review notes R-5 and R-6).
6. Whether the bar on sublicensing the Partner License, and teammates' licenses, should expressly allow end-user use of products (PA 4.3).
7. Whether, when Partner owns the work, it should see poster session materials a set time before the event (PA 4.5).
8. The limit on liability between Partner and students, and whether confidentiality breaches are excepted (PA 5.4).

Points not yet raised in a note: "the same terms as its other student founders" in the buyout (CA 5.5(c)) may need to say whether it covers vesting and work duties, and Alternative 1 assumes the founders received equal amounts. The PA also says nothing about the effect of a missing instructor approval when Partner certifies one; as in v0.5, the certification is Partner's warranty.
