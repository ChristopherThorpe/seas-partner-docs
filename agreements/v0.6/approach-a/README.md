# Approach A: Course Agreement and Project Agreement

## Summary

Approach A splits the agreements by audience. Everyone in a course offering, every student and every member of the Course Staff, signs one **Course Agreement**, and its Signers agree with each other. It states once every rule that protects other projects (Inadvertent Disclosures, other projects' Course Private Information, others' confidential information, and generative AI), the Course Staff's role, Teammates' rights (including the buyout of an inactive co-owner), a right to purchase work that a Partner owns and leaves unused, reassignment, Harvard's position, Partners' protection from claims, and the Confidentiality Period. It ends with a one-page **Project Election (No Partner)**, **Harvard's approval** as Exhibit A, and **default procedures** for the buyout and the right to purchase as Exhibit B. Each project with a Partner, which may be a company, another organization, or an individual, then has a **Project Agreement**: a cover page holding every choice and blank, with defaults printed beside each choice, followed by six sections of standard terms that are the same for every project. Partner and every student on the Project are parties to each other. Project Agreement Section 1.2 ties it to the Course Agreement: it names the version, binds Partner to the seven Course Agreement sections that concern it, and binds every student Signer to the course-wide rules and Teammates' rights even if they never signed the Course Agreement. If the two conflict, the Course Agreement governs.

The drafting instructions aimed for about 2,000 to 3,000 words for the Course Agreement with its forms, and 3,000 to 4,000 for the standard terms (these targets are not in the brief). Both are over. The owner's simplification (R14 to R17) cut Sections 1 to 8 to about 4,700 words. Round 4 (R18 to R26) added back about 160, for the limit on transfers by every later owner, the unreachable-phone rule, notices to Partners, and one definition of commercial use, so Sections 1 to 8 are about 4,860: the buyout about 390, the right to purchase about 360, and the notice rules about 330. With its forms and Exhibit B (about 545), the Course Agreement is about 5,860 words. The standard terms fell to about 4,290, because PA 4.5(g) and 6.2 now point to the Course Agreement. Cutting further would mean dropping a decision.

## Strengths

- **Each course-wide rule is stated once.** Every student and member of the Course Staff signs it and can enforce it directly. The v0.5 third-party beneficiary list for students, Exhibit C box 1, and Exhibit D's references to about twenty sections all disappear.
- **The Partner's document is short and predictable.** One cover page holds every choice, with defaults, and each IP choice is one complete subsection (PA 4.3 to 4.5). Everything the PA takes from the Course Agreement is in one section (PA 1.2).
- **Teammates' rights are the same for every project,** with or without a Partner, in one place (CA Section 5), including the accounting waiver that makes shared ownership workable.
- **Protection runs both ways.** Harvard and the Course Staff are protected from claims (CA 7.4), and so are Partners (CA 7.5), and Partner and the students release each other (PA 5.3). Exceptions beyond a Partner's own breach are left to counsel, with candidates listed in the note after CA 7.5.
- **Projects without a Partner** need only a one-page form; their terms sit next to the rules they depend on.
- **Harvard signs once,** and its approval travels with every Course Agreement as Exhibit A. The informal exception and Harvard's prior rights are stated where students read them (CA 7.2 and 7.3).
- **The cover page suits the document generator:** every choice is a labeled check box, almost all with defaults printed beside them.

## Weaknesses

- **Two signatures per student per project.** PA 1.2 binds a student Signer to the course-wide rules and Teammates' rights even without the Course Agreement, but the Course Staff still need to check that the roster is complete.
- **The PA is not self-contained for a Partner.** Partner agrees to seven Course Agreement sections and its definitions (PA 1.2), so it must receive the Course Agreement, with Exhibit A, with the PA.
- **Partners and Student Companies rely on third-party beneficiary rights** to enforce the Course Agreement (CA 8.3), and whether Harvard is bound to CA 7.5 at all is bracketed for counsel.
- **Partners' protection is stated in two places for student Signers:** CA 7.5 covers Harvard, the Course Staff, and every student, and PA 5.3 holds the mutual release between Partner and its students. They use the same formula and the same bracketed exceptions, but counsel should check them together.
- **A large multi-party agreement** signed at different times raises accession questions (note at CA 1.1). Changing the Course Agreement mid-offering needs every Signer (CA 8.4), so in practice fixes wait for the next offering unless counsel allows a corrected version.
- **Both documents are above their target lengths** (see "Length").
- **The cover page uses terms defined in the standard terms or the Course Agreement** (for example, Confidential Information and Partner Information), as cover-page agreements usually do.

## Open questions

The notes for counsel in the documents ask:

1. Whether later Course Agreement Signers are bound to, and may enforce against, earlier ones, and whether CA 8.4 should allow a corrected version for later Signers (CA 1.1).
2. Whether a government Partner needs a different form (CA 1.2).
3. Who counts as the SEAS community, and whether attendees must be told (CA 1.3; review note R-8).
4. Whether the reassignment assignment satisfies 17 U.S.C. § 204(a) and 35 U.S.C. § 261 (CA 2.3).
5. Whether a Course Staff member's promise not to assert rights works, given that Harvard may own employees' work (CA 4.1).
6. The buyout: enforceability, including the present assignment under § 204(a) and § 261, the power of attorney in Exhibit B, and adhesion for students who had to sign to take the Course; and securities and tax treatment (CA 5.5).
7. The right to purchase: enforceability against a Partner and every later owner; how the assignment is recorded; "equity typically granted to advisors" as a standard; securities and tax treatment; and how much of Exhibit B helps beyond good-faith negotiation (CA 5.7).
8. The gross negligence and willful misconduct carve-outs for Harvard and the Course Staff (CA 7.4; review notes R-5 and R-6).
9. Partner's protection: its exceptions beyond Partner's own breach (candidates: claims under a Separate Agreement; IP and confidentiality claims, including use beyond the rights granted; and personal claims); whether the releases bind students who must sign to take the Course, including students under 18; which claims cannot be released in advance; whether Partners are protected from Harvard's claims, and how Harvard would be bound; and consistency with PA 5.3 and 5.4 (CA 7.5).
10. How students under 18 sign (CA 8.4).
11. Whether the bar on sublicensing the Partner License, and Teammates' licenses, should expressly allow end-user use of products (PA 4.3).
12. The limit on liability between Partner and the Signers, and whether confidentiality breaches are excepted (PA 5.4).
