# Approach A: Course Agreement and Project Agreement

## Summary

Approach A splits the agreements by audience. Everyone in a course offering, every student and every member of the Course Staff, signs one **Course Agreement**, and its Signers agree with each other. It states once every rule that protects other projects (Inadvertent Disclosures, other projects' Course Private Information, others' confidential information, and generative AI), the Course Staff's role, Teammates' rights (including the bracketed buyout), the buyback from a Partner that leaves owned work unused, reassignment, Harvard's position, Partners' protection from claims, and the Confidentiality Period. It ends with a one-page **Project Election (No Partner)** and, as Exhibit A, **Harvard's approval**. Each project with a Partner, which may be a company, another organization, or an individual, then has a **Project Agreement**: a cover page holding every choice and blank, with defaults printed beside each choice, followed by six sections of standard terms that are the same for every project. Partner and every student on the Project are parties to each other. Project Agreement Section 1.2 ties it to the Course Agreement: it names the version, binds Partner to the seven Course Agreement sections that concern it, and binds every student Signer to the course-wide rules and Teammates' rights even if they never signed the Course Agreement. If the two conflict, the Course Agreement governs.

## Who signs what, and when

| Stakeholder | Signs | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Every student | Course Agreement (signature roster), confirming they have read the Student Guide | Once per course offering, before project work begins, or on enrolling later |
| Student on a project with a Partner | That project's Project Agreement (cover page) | Before receiving Partner information or starting material work; a later joiner signs a copy of the joinder page |
| Students on a project without a Partner | One Project Election (No Partner), at the end of the Course Agreement | Before starting material work; a later joiner signs it on joining |
| Course Staff (instructors, teaching fellows, teaching assistants, and course assistants) | Course Agreement (signature roster), in a personal capacity | Once per course offering, before project work begins, or on joining |
| Course Staff who need a project's Confidential Information | That project's Project Agreement (cover page or joinder page) | Before receiving Confidential Information |
| Course instructor | Nothing beyond the Course Agreement. Permits projects without a Partner, and approves any choice other than Open Science and any individual project, in writing or by email | At signing and before any later change |
| Partner (its authorized officer, or Partner itself if an individual) | Project Agreement; any change to it | Before project work begins; on any change |
| Partner Contacts | Nothing. Act for Partner by email (consents, generative AI tools, Enabling License, edits, credit changes); one may acknowledge joinders | During the Project |
| Student Company | Signs nothing to use Teammates' licenses. Gives written promises: to keep a bought-out Teammate's commitments (Course Agreement Section 5.5(d)), and to pay a Partner's costs with a buyback notice (Course Agreement Section 5.7(c)). Agrees in writing to be bound before receiving FSL interests (Project Agreement Section 4.4(f); Course Agreement Section 6.4(c)). May enforce what benefits it (Course Agreement Section 8.3) | When it acts |
| Harvard (through OTD, SEAS, or OGC) | Harvard's approval | Once per template version, for the listed courses; a copy is attached to each Course Agreement as Exhibit A |

The drafting instructions aimed for about 2,000 to 3,000 words for the Course Agreement with its forms, and 3,000 to 4,000 for the standard terms (these targets are not in the brief). Both are now over. The Course Agreement, at about 4,570 words with its forms, carries Teammates' rights, the buyout, and the new buyback (about 1,210 words in Section 5), the no-partner terms (about 480), and the new Partner's protection. The standard terms grew by about 270 words, mostly the power of attorney, the buyback and Harvard terms in Section 4.5, and the mutual release. Cutting further would mean dropping a decision.

## Strengths

- **Each course-wide rule is stated once.** Every student and member of the Course Staff signs it and can enforce it directly. The v0.5 third-party beneficiary list for students, Exhibit C box 1, and Exhibit D's references to about twenty sections all disappear.
- **The Partner's document is short and predictable.** One cover page holds every choice, with defaults, and each IP choice is one complete subsection (PA 4.3 to 4.5). Everything the PA takes from the Course Agreement is in one section (PA 1.2).
- **Teammates' rights are the same for every project,** with or without a Partner, in one place (CA Section 5), including the accounting waiver that makes shared ownership workable.
- **Protection runs both ways.** Harvard and the Course Staff are protected from claims (CA 7.4), and so are Partners (CA 7.5), and Partner and the students release each other (PA 5.3).
- **Projects without a Partner** need only a one-page form; their terms sit next to the rules they depend on.
- **Harvard signs once,** and its approval travels with every Course Agreement as Exhibit A. The informal exception and Harvard's prior rights are stated where students read them (CA 7.2 and 7.3).
- **The cover page suits the document generator:** every choice is a labeled check box, almost all with defaults printed beside them.

## Weaknesses

- **Two signatures per student per project.** PA 1.2 binds a student Signer to the course-wide rules and Teammates' rights even without the Course Agreement, but the Course Staff still need to check that the roster is complete.
- **The PA is not self-contained for a Partner.** Partner agrees to seven Course Agreement sections and its definitions (PA 1.2), so it must receive the Course Agreement, with Exhibit A, with the PA.
- **Partners and Student Companies rely on third-party beneficiary rights** to enforce the Course Agreement (CA 8.3), and Harvard is bound to CA 7.5 only through its approval.
- **Partners' protection is stated in two places for student Signers:** CA 7.5 covers Harvard, the Course Staff, and every student, and PA 5.3 holds the mutual release between Partner and its students. They use the same exceptions, but counsel should check them together.
- **A large multi-party agreement** signed at different times raises accession questions (note at CA 1.1). Changing the Course Agreement mid-offering needs every Signer (CA 8.4), so in practice fixes wait for the next offering unless counsel allows a corrected version.
- **Both documents are above their target lengths** (see "Length").
- **The cover page uses terms defined in the standard terms or the Course Agreement** (for example, Confidential Information and Partner Information), as cover-page agreements usually do.

## Open questions

The notes for counsel in the documents ask:

1. Whether later Course Agreement Signers are bound to, and may enforce against, earlier ones, and whether CA 8.4 should allow a corrected version for later Signers (CA 1.1).
2. Who counts as the SEAS community, and whether attendees must be told (CA 1.3; review note R-8).
3. Whether the reassignment assignment satisfies 17 U.S.C. § 204(a) and 35 U.S.C. § 261 (CA 2.3).
4. Whether a Course Staff member's promise not to assert rights works, given that Harvard may own employees' work (CA 4.1).
5. The buyout: which price alternative; whether to keep paragraph (f); enforceability against a holdout and against students who had to sign to take the Course; the present assignment under § 204(a) and § 261; the power of attorney; and securities and tax treatment (CA 5.5).
6. The buyback: enforceability against a Partner, patent applications in prosecution, and recordation (CA 5.7).
7. The gross negligence and willful misconduct carve-outs for Harvard and the Course Staff (CA 7.4; review notes R-5 and R-6).
8. Partner's protection: keeping CA 7.5 consistent with PA 5.3 and 5.4, and Harvard's agreement to it through Exhibit A (CA 7.5).
9. How students under 18 sign (CA 8.4).
10. Whether the bar on sublicensing the Partner License, and Teammates' licenses, should expressly allow end-user use of products (PA 4.3).
11. The limit on liability between Partner and the Signers, and whether confidentiality breaches are excepted (PA 5.4).

For the owner: whether to keep the bracketed rule in PA 3.1 that gives Partner a copy [10] business days before a Private Course Event (note after PA 4.5; it changes register item D3), and the bracketed capstone option in CA 2.4(b).

Points not yet raised in a note:

- "the same terms as its other student founders" in the buyout (CA 5.5(c)) may need to say whether it covers vesting and work duties, and Alternative 1 assumes the founders received equal amounts.
- The buyback (CA 5.7) does not say what happens if two Student Companies of the same project give notice.
- The PA's publication review election has no default printed beside it, because the owner's text omits one; under the cover page's general rule, a blank box means no review.
- The Student Guide's buyout steps do not yet reflect the mutual consent to join (CA 5.5(c)), or a Teammate's choice between competing notices (CA 5.5(g)); the owner's edits did not list them for the guide.
- The PA says nothing about the effect of a missing instructor approval when Partner certifies one; as in v0.5, the certification is Partner's warranty.
