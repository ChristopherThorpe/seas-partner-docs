## 1. Who signs and why

**1.1 Signers.** Every student in the Course, and every member of the "**Course Staff**" (the Course's instructors, teaching fellows, teaching assistants, and course assistants), must sign this agreement before project work begins, or on joining the Course. Each person who signs (a "**signer**") makes this agreement with every other signer, whenever each signs. Course Staff sign this agreement and any Project Agreement in a personal capacity, not for President and Fellows of Harvard College ("**Harvard**"). A signer's "**End Date**" is the earlier of the end of the Duration and the date they stop taking part in the Course or, for duties to a particular project, in that project.

> **Note for counsel:** Please confirm that each later signer is bound to, and may enforce the promises of, every earlier signer.

**1.2 Projects.** A project may have a sponsoring company (its "**partner**"), which signs an agreement with the project's students in the form Harvard has approved (a "**Project Agreement**"). Students on a project without a partner sign the Project Election at the end of this agreement (a "**Project Election**"). A project's Project Agreement or Project Election is its "**Project Terms**". Students on the same project are "**teammates**". "**Project Work Product**" is the results, reports, presentations, posters, videos, software, data, designs, and other work that a project's students create for it. It excludes materials that others own, such as open-source software, a partner's materials, or an employer's code, which keep their own owners and licenses.

**1.3 Course Community.** "**Course Private Information**" is information that a project's Project Terms make Course Private Information. It may be shared only within the "**Course Community**": (a) students enrolled in the Course during the Duration; (b) the Course Staff; (c) Harvard faculty and staff who support the Course; and (d) [members of the SEAS community, meaning the students, faculty, and staff of SEAS,] and invited guests, while attending a "**Private Course Event**", meaning a class, presentation, poster session, video showcase, project review, or similar Course event that is not open to the public. A partner may also mark information as "**Confidential Information**" under its Project Agreement, and only that agreement's signers may receive it.

> **Note for counsel (review note R-8):** Please advise who counts as the SEAS community (for example, cross-registered students, postdocs, and alumni), how that is determined, and whether attendees at Private Course Events must be told that Course Private Information is shown.

**1.4 Student Guide.** Before choosing a project, each student must confirm, on the signature roster or by email to the Course Staff, that they have read the SEAS Student Guide for course projects. The guide explains these agreements but is not part of them, and the agreements govern if they differ.

**1.5 Project Terms and this agreement.** Project Terms set their project's choices and govern that project where they differ from this agreement. They may not reduce the protection this agreement gives other projects, their partners, or their students.

## 2. Open Science and choosing a project

**2.1 Open Science.** The Course's baseline is Open Science. A project is "**Open Science**" if (a) no information about it is restricted (the "Open" information choice); (b) its students own their work; (c) its software is licensed under the MIT License, the Apache License 2.0, or both; and (d) its other Project Work Product is licensed under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International ("**CC BY 4.0**") or Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International ("**CC BY-SA 4.0**"). Any other choice, and any individual project, needs the Course instructor's approval in writing (email is enough), at signing and for any later change. The instructor does not sign.

**2.2 Choosing a project.** A student may ask to be assigned to an Open Science project or to do a project without a partner. A student must sign a project's Project Terms before receiving its information or starting material work on it.

**2.3 Reassignment.** A student may ask to move to another project, including an Open Science project or a project without a partner, for any reason, such as not accepting their project's Project Terms. The Course Staff must honor the request. Work the student already contributed to the project they leave stays under its Project Terms, as if the student had signed them on the same terms as its other students, and the student hereby assigns or licenses that work accordingly. The student must keep that project's partner information within the Course Community as Course Private Information, and its Confidential Information as its Project Agreement requires of signers.

**2.4 Capstones.** If the Course is a capstone for a degree program, a student may decline every partner project's Project Terms and do a project without a partner or, where the program allows, write a thesis instead of taking the Course.

## 3. Rules for everyone in the Course

These rules bind every signer and protect every project, with or without a partner.

**3.1 Inadvertent Disclosures.** An "**Inadvertent Disclosure**" happens when someone receives, in connection with the Course, confidential information not meant for them, for example from a classmate's shared folder, a misdirected message, or another project's presentation. A signer who receives one must use reasonable care to (a) tell the person who disclosed it; (b) not copy, forward, or otherwise use it; and (c) permanently delete every copy, including notes, files, and photos. "**Reasonable care**" means what a reasonable person in the recipient's position would do, given their role, the information, and when they learned it was not meant for them. Taking steps (a) to (c) promptly after learning of the disclosure is reasonable care. No one is liable for merely receiving an Inadvertent Disclosure, but this protection does not cover intentional use or disclosure afterwards. This Section 3.1 does not cover Course Private Information shared within the Course Community.

**3.2 Other projects' Course Private Information.** A signer must keep the Course Private Information of any project they are not on within the Course Community, use it only for the Course, and never pass it to a partner, unless that project's partner and students (or, for a project without a partner, its students) consent in writing. A project's students may show its Course Private Information at a Private Course Event. They must use reasonable care to ask anyone seen recording it to stop and delete the recording, but are not responsible for what attendees who have not signed this agreement do with it.

**3.3 Others' confidential information.** A signer must not share with anyone in the Course, or use in a project, confidential information that they are not authorized to share or use, such as a current or former employer's.

**3.4 Generative AI.** Signers must use generative AI tools for Course work through their Harvard accounts and Harvard's tools, unless the Course Staff allow others or a partner asks its project's students to use the partner's own tools at the partner's expense. A partner's Course Private Information and Confidential Information may go only into tools that the partner has approved in writing or by email.

## 4. Course Staff

**4.1 Role.** The Course Staff advise, coordinate, and evaluate projects. They must not contribute intellectual property to any project, and their evaluations, grades, and feedback are not Project Work Product. If a member of the Course Staff contributes material anyway, they claim no ownership of it and grant whatever rights they hold in it on the same terms as the project's students grant theirs.

> **Note for counsel:** Harvard may own what its employees create. Please confirm whether this fallback grant works without Harvard's consent.

**4.2 Education records.** The Course Staff must not share students' grades or other education records, as defined in the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (20 U.S.C. § 1232g), with any partner.

**4.3 Confidential Information.** A member of the Course Staff may receive a project's Confidential Information only by signing its Project Agreement, and has no duties of a signer under a Project Agreement they have not signed.

## 5. Teammates' rights

**5.1 Scope.** Sections 5.2 to 5.5 apply to Project Work Product that a team's students own or co-own, with or without a partner, but not to work that Project Terms assign, or later assign, to a partner. Any limit in the Project Terms on how the work may be licensed, such as under the Functional Source License, Version 1.1 ("**FSL**"), also applies to the licenses in this Section 5. Section 5.6 applies to every team.

**5.2 Teammate license.** Each student on a team grants each teammate a perpetual, worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free license, irrevocable except under Section 5.5, under the student's rights in the team's Project Work Product, to use, copy, modify, distribute, make, sell, and otherwise exploit it for any purpose, subject to the project's confidentiality terms. The license extends to the teammate's Student Company. It may not otherwise be transferred, or be sublicensed without the granting student's written consent.

**5.3 Student Company.** A student's "**Student Company**" is a company that the student founded or co-founded, or in which students on the project together hold at least 25% of the voting equity, and that, for a project with a partner, no competitor of that partner controls, in each case when the company first uses or receives the work.

**5.4 Separate companies and later work.** If teammates start separate companies, each company keeps its license. Course work stays non-exclusive, except under Section 5.5. What a student or Student Company creates after the student's End Date is its own.

**5.5 Buyout ("use it or lose it").**

(a) This Section 5.5 applies only to Project Work Product that a team's students own and that has no public license. It does not apply to work owned jointly with a partner, work under an FSL license, or work a partner owns.

(b) After a teammate's End Date, a Student Company of one or more of the team's students that is actively developing the work may give the teammate written notice, if the teammate is not then using the work commercially, personally or through a company. The notice must describe the company and the terms on which its student founders hold their equity.

(c) The teammate may join the company on the same terms as its other student founders, by written notice within [60] days after receiving the notice.

(d) If the teammate does not join, the teammate must assign their ownership share in the work to the company for the price in paragraph (e), and hereby does so, effective when the company pays the price or, for a price payable later, gives the teammate its signed written promise to pay it. The teammate must sign documents reasonably needed to record the assignment, at the company's expense.

(e) The price is [Alternative 1: the greater of (1) what each founding student received for assigning their own rights in the work to the company and (2) [0.01]% of the company's fully diluted equity] [Alternative 2: [number]% of the proceeds of any sale of the company or of substantially all its assets, up to $[amount], paid at the sale].

(f) [The assignment ends the teammate's license under Section 5.2 to the work, except for portfolio, academic, and other noncommercial use.]

> **Note for counsel:** Choose one price alternative, and whether to keep paragraph (f), before use. Without paragraph (f), the company cannot get exclusive rights. Please confirm that the buyout is enforceable, including the present assignment against a teammate who will not sign anything, and advise on the securities and tax treatment of equity or sale proceeds for a teammate who does not join.

**5.6 Copies and credit.**

(a) Before a student's End Date, and before Course computing resources holding Project Work Product are shut down, the student must make reasonable efforts to ensure that each teammate receives copies of the Project Work Product the teammate is entitled to.

(b) Before submitting material about Project Work Product for publication, a student must offer each teammate co-authorship, unless the material covers substantial work done after the Duration, and must credit any teammate who is not a co-author in the acknowledgments, unless they ask not to be named. Co-authors must get reasonable time to review the draft and request changes, and other teammates reasonable time to review it and comment. A teammate may be asked to sign a reasonable nondisclosure agreement before receiving a confidential draft.

(c) Paragraph (b) does not apply to coursework, class presentations, posters, videos, or other private academic uses. During the Duration, a project's students and its partner, if any, may change paragraph (b) for their project in writing, including by email.

## 6. Projects without a partner

**6.1 Project Election.** All students on a project without a partner, individual or team, sign one Project Election before starting material work, and a later joiner signs it on joining. On it, they certify that the Course instructor has approved, in writing or by email, any individual project and every choice that is not Open Science. A blank choice, more than one checked choice (other than the MIT License and the Apache License 2.0 together), or a team-only choice on an individual project gives way to the default.

**6.2 Information.** Under "Open" (the default), nothing about the project is restricted. Under "Course Private", what the students share about the project with classmates and the Course Staff is Course Private Information. That choice binds other signers, not the project's own students.

**6.3 Licenses.** Each student licenses their rights in the project's software, to everyone who receives a copy, under the MIT License (the default), the Apache License 2.0, both (each recipient chooses), or another named license. Each student licenses their rights in other Project Work Product, in the same way, under CC BY 4.0 (the default), CC BY-SA 4.0, or another named license. A category marked "None" has no public license. An individual student who marks "None" for both keeps all rights. A team that marks "None" for a category keeps its rights in it, subject to Section 5.

**6.4 FSL (team projects only).** If the students choose FSL-1.1-MIT or FSL-1.1-ALv2 (FSL with the MIT License or the Apache License 2.0, respectively, as its future license):

(a) each student assigns each other student an undivided interest in their rights in the software, so that they own it jointly, and together they license it under that FSL license;

(b) each student may use the software for any purpose, personally or through their Student Company, and no such use is a competing use under the FSL license;

(c) no student may license or distribute the software in any other way, except to their Student Company, unless all of the students agree in writing;

(d) no student owes another an accounting of profits;

(e) one or more students may assign their interests to their Student Company if it first agrees in writing to be bound as they are; and

(f) a majority of the students may choose, and by written notice to all of them replace, one student or Student Company as the "**FSL Representative**", who manages releases (naming the students, or their Student Company, as copyright holders) and any enforcement of the FSL license for all of them.

**6.5 Changes.** The choices may be changed only in a writing signed by all of the project's current students, who certify the instructor's approval of any choice that is not Open Science. A change does not take back rights that others already received.

## 7. Harvard

**7.1 Not a party.** Harvard is not a party to this agreement or to any Project Terms, and has no obligations under them. Harvard has approved the forms of this agreement and the Project Agreement, including every choice in them, for the Course, in an approval kept on file at SEAS.

**7.2 Harvard's rights come first.** This agreement and Project Terms do not assign or license any right that Harvard owns, or amend any agreement between Harvard and a partner or other third party about the Course or a project (a "**Separate Agreement**"). Every signer's rights are subject to Harvard's rights under its policies and any Separate Agreement, including rights from faculty-sponsored research. As between Harvard and a partner, a Separate Agreement governs if it conflicts with a Project Agreement.

**7.3 Informal exception.** Harvard's Office of Technology Development applies a conditional informal exception to Harvard's Statement of Policy in Regard to Intellectual Property for coursework. If neither box under "Harvard's rights in students' work" is checked, Harvard claims no ownership of what a student creates in the Course, as long as the student uses only Harvard-administered resources provided generally to students in the Course. Some Harvard research computing resources could end the exception. If either box is checked, the exception does not apply.

No signer may assert such a claim. ## 8. General

**8.1 Confidentiality Period.** Every confidentiality duty under this agreement and any Project Terms, including for other projects' information, lasts until five years after the person's End Date (the "**Confidentiality Period**"), and, for a trade secret, as long as it remains a trade secret. The rest of this agreement continues after the Course ends.

**8.2 Exclusions and permitted disclosures.** These apply to every confidentiality duty under this agreement and any Project Terms.

(a) A duty does not cover information that the recipient can show is or becomes public through no breach, was known to the recipient without restriction before receipt, was received from someone free to disclose it, or was developed independently without using it.

(b) Anyone may disclose information as a law, regulation, subpoena, or court order requires, after giving its owner prompt notice, where the law allows, and reasonable help to seek protection at the owner's expense.

(c) Nothing in this agreement or any Project Terms prohibits reporting a possible violation of law to a government agency, or any other disclosure the law protects. Under 18 U.S.C. § 1833(b), an individual is not liable under any federal or state trade secret law for disclosing a trade secret in confidence to a government official or a lawyer solely to report or investigate a suspected violation of law, or in a document filed under seal in a lawsuit or other proceeding.

**8.3 Enforcement.** Signers, including teammates, may enforce this agreement against each other directly. Each partner of a project in the Course may enforce Sections 2.3, 3, and 4.3 as they protect that partner's information, and Harvard may enforce Sections 7.2 and 7.4. No one else has rights under this agreement. A breach of Section 3 or 5 may cause irreparable harm, so anyone entitled to enforce it may seek an injunction. Each signer is responsible only for their own acts.

**8.4 Law, signatures, and interpretation.** Massachusetts law governs this agreement, without regard to conflict-of-laws rules, and the state and federal courts in Massachusetts have exclusive jurisdiction over disputes about it. Signatures may be electronic, including through Adobe Acrobat Sign, and counterparts together form one agreement. Each signer confirms that they have reached the age of majority, have read this agreement, have had the chance to ask questions and consult advisors, and sign voluntarily. This agreement may be changed only in a writing signed by every signer, and no signer may assign it. "Including" means "including without limitation". An unenforceable term is enforced as far as the law allows, and the rest stays in effect.
