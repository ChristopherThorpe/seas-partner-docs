## 1. Who signs and why

**1.1 Signers.** Every student in the Course and every member of the "**Course Staff**" (its instructors, teaching fellows, teaching assistants, and course assistants) must sign this agreement before project work begins or on joining the Course. Each person who signs (a "**Signer**") makes this agreement with every other Signer, whenever each signs. Course Staff sign this agreement and any Project Agreement in a personal capacity, not for President and Fellows of Harvard College ("**Harvard**"). A Signer's "**End Date**" is the earlier of the end of the Duration and the date they stop taking part in the Course or, for duties to a particular project, in that project.

> **Note for counsel:** Please confirm that each later Signer is bound to, and may enforce the promises of, every earlier Signer, and advise whether Section 8.4 should allow a corrected version for later Signers without every earlier Signer's signature.

**1.2 Projects.** A project may have a sponsor (its "**Partner**"): a company, another organization (such as a nonprofit or a government agency), or an individual. The Partner and the project's students sign a "**Project Agreement**" in the form Harvard has approved. Students on a project without a Partner sign the "**Project Election**" that follows the signature roster. Either is the project's "**Project Terms**". Each student on a project is a "**Teammate**" of every other student on it. "**Project Work Product**" is the results, reports, presentations, posters, videos, software, data, designs, and other work that a project's students create for it, except materials that others own, such as open-source software, a Partner's materials, or a current or former employer's code, which keep their own owners and licenses.

No student in the Course may be or control a Partner[ without Harvard's written approval], and no instructor of the Course without Harvard's written consent. Any other member of the Course Staff may be or control a Partner only if they recuse themselves from evaluating that Partner's project and do not access its students' grades or evaluations. Material they provide as or for that Partner is the Partner's, and Sections 4.1, 4.3, and 7.4 do not apply to what they do as or for it. One who is the Partner signs its Project Agreement as the Partner and has a Partner's duties; one who only controls a Partner does not take them on personally.

> **Note for counsel:** Please advise whether a government Partner needs a different form, for example on governing law, releases, public-records laws, and rights in government-funded work.

**1.3 Course Community.** "**Course Private Information**" is information that a project's Project Terms make Course Private Information. It may be shared only within the "**Course Community**": (a) students enrolled in the Course during the Duration; (b) the Course Staff; (c) Harvard faculty and staff who support the Course; and (d) [members of the SEAS community, meaning the students, faculty, and staff of SEAS, and] invited guests, while attending a "**Private Course Event**", meaning a class, presentation, poster session, video showcase, project review, or similar Course event not open to the public. A Partner may also mark information as "**Confidential Information**" under its Project Agreement, which says who may receive it.

> **Note for counsel:** (Review note R-8.) Please advise who counts as the SEAS community (for example, cross-registered students, postdocs, and alumni), how that is determined, and whether attendees at Private Course Events must be told that Course Private Information is shown.

**1.4 Student Guide.** Before choosing a project, each student must confirm on the signature roster that they have read the *Student Guide to Course Project Agreements*. The guide explains these agreements but is not part of them, and the agreements govern if they differ.

**1.5 Conflicts.** If Project Terms conflict with this agreement, this agreement governs.

## 2. Open Science and choosing a project

**2.1 Open Science.** A project is "**Open Science**", the Course's default, if (a) nothing about it is restricted (the "Open" information choice); (b) its students own their work; (c) its software is licensed under the MIT License, the Apache License 2.0, or both; and (d) its other Project Work Product is licensed under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International ("**CC BY 4.0**") or Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International ("**CC BY-SA 4.0**"). Other choices include the Functional Source License, Version 1.1 ("**FSL**"), as FSL-1.1-MIT or FSL-1.1-ALv2. Any other choice, and any individual project, needs the Course instructor's approval in writing (email is enough), at signing and for any later change. The instructor does not sign.

**2.2 Choosing a project.** At a student's request, the Course Staff must assign the student to an Open Science project. A student may do a project without a Partner if the Course instructor permits it. A student must sign a project's Project Terms before receiving its information or starting material work on it.

**2.3 Reassignment.** A student may ask to move to another project with different Project Terms, including an Open Science project or a project without a Partner, for any reason, such as not accepting their project's Project Terms. The Course Staff must make a good-faith effort to accommodate the request, and must honor a request to move to an Open Science project. The Course Staff need not assign any student to any specific project. Work the student already contributed to the project they leave stays under its Project Terms as they stood when the student left, as if the student had signed them, and the student hereby assigns or licenses it accordingly. The student must keep that project's Course Private Information and Confidential Information as its signers must.

> **Note for counsel:** Please confirm that this assignment, which may run to a Partner that is not a party to this agreement under Project Terms the student did not sign, satisfies 17 U.S.C. § 204(a) and 35 U.S.C. § 261.

**2.4 Capstones.** If the Course is a capstone for a degree program, a student may (a) write a thesis instead of taking the Course, if permitted by the degree program[, or (b) decline every Partner project's Project Terms and do a project without a Partner, without the Course instructor's permission under Section 2.2 or approval of an individual project under Section 2.1].

## 3. Rules for everyone in the Course

**3.1 Inadvertent Disclosures.** An "**Inadvertent Disclosure**" happens when someone receives, in connection with the Course, confidential information not meant for them. A Signer who receives one must use "**Reasonable Care**" (what a reasonable person in their position would do, given their role, the information, and when they learned it was not meant for them) to (a) tell the person who disclosed it; (b) not copy, forward, or otherwise use it; and (c) permanently delete every copy, including notes, files, and photos. Taking steps (a) to (c) promptly after learning of the disclosure is Reasonable Care. No one is liable merely for receiving an Inadvertent Disclosure. Course Private Information shared within the Course Community is not an Inadvertent Disclosure.

**3.2 Other projects' Course Private Information.** A Signer must keep the Course Private Information of any project they are not on within the Course Community, use it only for the Course, and never pass it to a Partner. These limits do not apply where that project's students and Partner (if any) consent in writing. A project's students may show its Course Private Information at a Private Course Event, but must use Reasonable Care to ask anyone seen recording it to stop and delete the recording. They are not responsible for what attendees who are not Signers do with it.

**3.3 Others' confidential information.** A Signer must not share with any Partner or anyone else in the Course, or use in a project, confidential information that they are not authorized to share or use, such as a current or former employer's.

**3.4 Generative AI.** Signers who use generative AI tools for Course work must use tools Harvard has approved, through their Harvard accounts and Harvard's tools. The Course Staff may allow other tools, and a Partner may ask its project's students to use the Partner's own tools at the Partner's expense. A Partner's Course Private Information and Confidential Information may go only into tools that the Partner has approved in writing or by email.

One who contributes material anyway must not assert any right in it against the project's students, its Partner, or anyone who receives the work under its Project Terms.

## 5. Teammates' rights

**5.1 Scope.** Sections 5.3 to 5.5 apply to Project Work Product that a team's students own or co-own, including under an FSL license, with or without a Partner, but not to work that a Partner owns or has owned outright. The licenses in this Section 5 are subject to any limit in the Project Terms on licensing the work. If Project Terms are changed to assign the work to a Partner, those licenses end in it only for students who sign the change. In this Section 5, use in any product, service, or business operation, including internal use, is commercial use.

**5.2 Student Company.** A student's "**Student Company**" is a company that the student founded or co-founded, or in which students on the project together hold at least 25% of the voting equity, and, for a project with a Partner, that no competitor of that Partner controls. These tests apply when the company first uses or receives the work.

**5.3 Teammate license.** Each student on a team grants each Teammate a perpetual, worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free license under the student's rights in the team's Project Work Product. The license is irrevocable except under Sections 5.1 and 5.5. It lets the Teammate and their Student Company use, copy, modify, make derivative works of, distribute, display, perform, make, have made, sell, offer to sell, import, and otherwise exploit the work for any purpose, subject to the project's confidentiality terms. It may not otherwise be transferred, or be sublicensed without the granting student's written consent. Except through their Student Company, no student may license the work beyond its Project Terms without the other owners' written consent. No student owes another an accounting of profits, or any other payment, for any use or licensing of the work, by the student or their Student Company, that this Section 5 or the Project Terms permit.

**5.4 Separate companies and later work.** If Teammates start separate companies, each company keeps its license. Course work stays non-exclusive, except under Section 5.5. What a student or Student Company creates after the student's End Date is its own.

**5.5 Buyout of an inactive co-owner.**

(a) This Section applies to Project Work Product that a team's students own and that has no public license. It does not apply to work under an FSL license or work a Partner owns.

(b) After a Teammate's End Date, a Student Company of one or more of the team's students that is actively developing the work may buy out that Teammate's rights in it. It may do so if, on the date of its notice, the Teammate is not using the work commercially, personally or through a company. Starting to use the work after the notice does not stop the buyout.

(c) If both agree, the Teammate may join the company instead.

(d) The company must offer the Teammate fully vested equity at least equal to the greater of (1) what each founding student received for their own rights in the work, and (2) what the company's initial advisors who don't work for it full time receive. Neither amount counts equity that vests as a founder works for the company. The company and the Teammate may agree on any other amount or form.

(e) The Teammate hereby assigns all of their rights in the work to the company, effective when the company tenders the price under Exhibit B. The assignment takes effect only if the company has first promised in writing to keep the Teammate's commitments about the work, such as the Partner's license and confidentiality. It ends the Teammate's rights in the work, except the right to describe and show their own contribution in a portfolio or academic setting, without distributing it.

(f) A dispute about the price does not delay the assignment. It is a claim for the difference only.

(g) A buyout is possible only while one group of the team's students is actively developing the work. If two or more groups are, there is no exclusivity to gain, so no one may use this Section, and each group keeps its license under Section 5.3 unless they agree otherwise.

(h) Exhibit B sets out the default process: what the notice must contain and show (including the founding students' and initial advisors' grants), the Teammate's 30 days to decide, cash at fair market value if the Teammate refuses equity, and signing recording papers for a Teammate who won't.

> **Note for counsel:** Please confirm that this buyout is enforceable: whether the present assignment in paragraph (e) satisfies 17 U.S.C. § 204(a) and 35 U.S.C. § 261 when the company may not yet exist; whether the power of attorney in Exhibit B is enforceable; and whether the buyout binds a student who had to sign this agreement to take the Course. Please also advise on the securities and tax treatment of the equity in paragraph (d).

**5.6 Copies and credit.**

(a) Before a student's End Date, and before Course computing resources holding Project Work Product shut down, the student must make reasonable efforts to get each Teammate copies of the Project Work Product the Teammate is entitled to.

(b) Before submitting material about Project Work Product for publication, a student must offer each Teammate co-authorship, unless the material covers substantial work done after the Duration. The student must credit any Teammate who is not a co-author in the acknowledgments, unless they ask not to be named. Co-authors must get reasonable time to review the draft and request changes, and other Teammates reasonable time to review it and comment. A Teammate may be asked to sign a reasonable nondisclosure agreement before receiving a confidential draft.

(c) Paragraph (b) does not apply to coursework, class presentations, posters, videos, or other private academic uses. During the Duration, all of a project's students together and, for a project with a Partner, at least one of its Partner Contacts may change the project's credit process, including under its Project Agreement. They may do so in writing, including by email.

**5.7 Right to purchase unused work.**

(a) This Section applies to Project Work Product that a Partner owns or has owned outright under the "Partner owns" choice, except Partner Background IP as defined in the Project Agreement. It binds every later owner of the work.

(b) From [2] years after the end of the Duration, the project's Partner or any of its students, alone or through an existing or prospective Student Company, may give notice that it wishes to purchase the work, but only if no one has used the work commercially during the [2] years before the notice. A patent application, provisional or not, is not use.

(c) The notice, and each proposal, goes to the owner, the Partner, and all of the project's students, with an email address for replies. Anyone entitled to buy may propose within 30 days after the first notice. Part 1 of Exhibit B is the default procedure.

(d) The buyer must pay the owner at least its documented out-of-pocket costs for the work, such as patent filing and outside legal fees, plus, if the buyer is or acts through a Student Company, equity in that company not less than that typically granted to its advisors.

(e) Patents and patent applications on the work transfer with it. If the owner is the Partner, it keeps a perpetual, non-exclusive, royalty-free license to use and exploit the work for any purpose, transferable only with its business. The purchase ends any exclusive license in the work, is subject to non-exclusive licenses granted before the notice, and gives no right to use or disclose Partner Information that the Project Agreement restricts.

(f) The owner must not transfer the work, or grant an exclusive license in it, unless the transferee or licensee first agrees in writing to be bound by this Section and Part 1 of Exhibit B. That agreement must be for the benefit of the project's Partner, its students, and their Student Companies, who may enforce it. The owner must notify them within 30 days, with a copy of that agreement and the transferee's or licensee's email address.

## 8. General

**8.1 Confidentiality Period.** Every confidentiality duty under this agreement and any Project Terms, including for other projects' information, lasts until five years after the person's End Date (the "**Confidentiality Period**"), and, for a trade secret, as long as it remains a trade secret. The rest of this agreement continues after the Course ends.

**8.2 Exclusions and permitted disclosures.** No confidentiality duty under this agreement or any Project Terms covers information that the recipient can show is or becomes public through no breach, was known to the recipient without restriction before receipt, was received from someone free to disclose it, or was developed independently without using it. Anyone may disclose information as a law, regulation, subpoena, or court order requires, after giving its owner prompt notice, where the law allows, and reasonable help to seek protection at the owner's expense. Nothing in this agreement or any Project Terms prohibits reporting a possible violation of law to a government agency, or any other disclosure the law protects. Under 18 U.S.C. § 1833(b), an individual is not liable under any federal or state trade secret law for disclosing a trade secret in confidence to a government official or a lawyer, solely to report or investigate a suspected violation of law. The same applies to a disclosure in a document filed under seal in a lawsuit or other proceeding.

**8.3 Enforcement.** Signers, including Teammates, may enforce this agreement against each other directly. Each Partner of a project in the Course may enforce Section 7.5; Sections 3 and 4.3 as they protect its information; and Sections 2.3, 4.1, and 5.7, and Part 1 of Exhibit B, as they apply to its project. A Student Company may enforce Sections 5.3 to 5.5, 5.7, and 6.4, and Exhibit B, as they benefit it. Harvard may enforce Sections 7.2 and 7.4. No one else has rights under this agreement, apart from rights under a public license granted under Section 6.3 or 6.4. A breach of Section 3 or 5 may cause irreparable harm, so anyone entitled to enforce it may seek an injunction. Each Signer is responsible only for their own acts.

**8.4 Law, signatures, and interpretation.** Massachusetts law governs this agreement, without regard to conflict-of-laws rules, and the state and federal courts in Massachusetts have exclusive jurisdiction over disputes about it. Signatures may be electronic, including through Adobe Acrobat Sign, and counterparts together form one agreement. Each Signer confirms that they have reached the age of majority, have read this agreement, have had the chance to ask questions and consult advisors, and sign voluntarily. This agreement may be changed only in a writing signed by every Signer, and no Signer may assign it. A reference to all, or a majority, of a project's students counts only that project's students, and a project's "current" students are those still taking part in it or, after the Duration, all of its students. "Including" means "including without limitation". An unenforceable term is enforced as far as the law allows, and the rest stays in effect.

## Exhibit B: Default procedures

This Exhibit B is part of the Course Agreement. Its procedures are defaults: in Part 1 the owner and the buyer, and in Part 2 the company and the Teammate, may agree in writing on something else. No such agreement may reduce the equity owed to an unsuccessful bidder without its consent. Notices follow Section 8.5.

### Part 1: Purchase of unused work (Section 5.7)

1. If only one person or group has given notice or a proposal when the 30 days in Section 5.7(c) end, they may buy the work at the floor in Section 5.7(d).
2. If more than one has, the owner selects one in its sole discretion, based in part on their proposals about how they intend to use the work and what they will pay, within [30] days after those 30 days end. If the owner selects no one in time, the first to give notice or a proposal meeting the floor may buy.
3. Each unsuccessful bidder that is a student or a group of students gets at least the equity the successful buyer offers the owner, whether or not the owner accepts it.
4. Within 30 days after the first notice, the owner gives everyone the notice went to a written statement of its documented out-of-pocket costs for the work.
5. The buyer, or its company, then prepares, signs, and gives the owner a written agreement to buy the work on at least the terms in Section 5.7(d), or on its selected proposal, and to be bound as Section 5.7(f) requires. If step 3 applies, the agreement also promises each unsuccessful bidder its equity, and that bidder may enforce the promise. Within 30 days after receiving the agreement, the owner must sign it and an assignment of the work, including patents and patent applications, keeping any license that Section 5.7(e) reserves.

### Part 2: Buyout of an inactive co-owner (Section 5.5)

1. The company's notice to the Teammate describes the company and its offer under Section 5.5(d), and shows the equity granted to its founding students and its initial advisors. It also states how cash would be tendered (for example, by bank transfer or a check sent to the Teammate's address on file), and gives an email address for replies.
2. Section 5.5(g) is tested on the date of the notice, as Section 5.5(b) is. The company's promise under Section 5.5(e) is for the benefit of the Teammate, the project's Partner (if any), and its other students, who may enforce it.
3. The Teammate has 30 days after the notice to accept the offer, to agree with the company to join it under Section 5.5(c), or to refuse.
4. If the Teammate refuses the equity, or does not accept it within those 30 days, the company tenders cash equal to the equity's fair market value instead, by the method its notice states. Tendering the equity or that cash is tendering the price under Section 5.5(e).
5. The Teammate must sign documents reasonably needed to record the assignment, at the company's expense. If the Teammate does not sign them within [15] business days after the company's written request, the Teammate hereby appoints the company as their attorney-in-fact, coupled with an interest, to sign them in the Teammate's name.
