# v0.6: three ways to structure the agreements

Three complete drafts carry the same decisions (`BRIEF.md`) in different document structures. Each was drafted, given a close read by an independent reviewer (`reviews/`), and revised. All three handle the 24 situations in `SCENARIOS.md`; each approach's README says how, section by section. The shared, non-binding `shared/student-guide.md` works with any of them.

**Recommendation: approach A, with the document generator leaving out the terms for choices a project didn't make.** Reasons are at the end.

## The three approaches

| | A: Course Agreement + Project Agreement | B: Tiered short forms | C: Course master + schedules |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Course-level document** | Course Agreement (about 4,960 words, including the one-page form for projects without a partner, and Harvard's approval attached as Exhibit A), signed by every student and staff member | Course Rules (about 2,280 words), acknowledged electronically by every student and staff member | Master agreement (about 6,360 words), signed by every student and staff member, and joined by each partner |
| **Project with a partner** | One Project Agreement: a cover page of choices (about 850 words) plus standard terms (about 4,300) | Open Science short form (about 2,310); add the Restricted Project Addendum (about 2,010) for any other choice | One Project Schedule (about 700), which the partner signs to join the master |
| **Project without a partner** | One-page Project Election, part of the Course Agreement | Team Project Form (about 790) | The same Project Schedule |
| **What a partner reads** | The Project Agreement, plus the Course Agreement sections it agrees to (listed in its Section 1.2) | About 3,100 words if Open Science; about 5,100 otherwise | The schedule and about two-thirds of the master |
| **What a student on a partner project signs** | Course Agreement and Project Agreement | Course Rules (click), short form, and any addendum | Master and schedule |
| **How partners are bound to course-wide rules** | By agreeing in the Project Agreement to named Course Agreement sections; they enforce as third-party beneficiaries | The same, through the short form | Directly, as parties to the master |
| **Changing a project's choices mid-course** | Sign a change to the cover page | Sign an addendum; nothing is re-signed | Sign a changed schedule |
| **Main risk** | Two documents must stay in step; a large agreement signed at different times by many people | Three documents cross-reference each other; electronic assent carries IP transfers | Partners may refuse to join one agreement with other companies, possibly competitors; no negotiation or mid-term correction |

All three keep the same substance: students are parties to each other; three information choices; IP as ownership plus public licenses; Open Science by default, with the instructor's approval for anything else and for every individual project; teammates' licenses with no duty to share profits, extending to a Student Company; the bracketed buyout; course-wide rules binding everyone; the five-year Confidentiality Period; and Harvard's approval (on file in B and C; attached to the Course Agreement as Exhibit A in A, at the owner's request).

## Common fixes from the reviews

The three independent reviews found the same weak points in every draft, and all were fixed:

- **The buyout didn't reliably transfer anything.** It now uses present words of assignment ("hereby assigns"), takes effect on payment or tender of the price, lets the company pay cash if the teammate refuses equity, gives the company a power of attorney to sign confirming papers, requires the company to agree to be bound, and settles competing notices. Counsel notes cover 17 U.S.C. § 204(a), 35 U.S.C. § 261, adhesion, securities, and tax.
- **Teammates' licenses didn't waive the co-owner's duty to share profits.** Each now says no student owes another an accounting.
- **Student Companies couldn't enforce the terms that benefit them.** They now can.
- **Partners weren't bound to keep other projects' Course Private Information** that they see at course events. They now are.
- **Course Staff contributions:** a TA who contributes anyway can't use their rights against the project. In A and B they promise not to assert those rights; in C they grant a license.

## Approach A after the owner's review

The owner chose approach A and has edited it in several rounds (`owner-edits/`), with two further independent reviews (`reviews/approach-a-review-2.md` and `approach-a-review-3.md`). The owner has answered the decisions in `owner-edits/approach-a-2026-11-06-decisions-to-confirm.md`; items R5 to R11 and the drafter's latest wording choices await confirmation. Approach A now also includes:

- a right to purchase work that a Partner owns and leaves unused;
- mutual protection for Partners, with exceptions beyond a Partner's own breach left to counsel;
- notices by email plus text or voicemail, repeated until acknowledged, with every individual and Partner Contact giving a telephone number;
- Partners that may be organizations or individuals;
- renamed information choices ("Open", "Open and Course Private", and "Open, Course Private, and Confidential");
- capitalized defined terms throughout.

The decisions below were written for the choice among the three approaches; some are now settled for A.

## Decisions for you

1. **Which structure** (A, B, or C, or a mix).
2. **The buyout:** which price alternative, whether the sale ends the seller's license, and the [60]-day period.
3. **Poster sessions when Partner owns the work or plans a patent:** A brackets a rule giving Partner a copy [10] business days before a private course event. This changes the v0.5 rule (Partner gets its copy when the poster is presented). B and C raise it as a question.
4. **A Student Company later acquired by a competitor of the partner:** should it lose its license or FSL exemption? All three keep v0.5's rule, which tests competitor control only when the company first uses the work.
5. **Changes need only the students still taking part.** All three now let a change go ahead without students who have left, and never bind them. This changes v0.5 register B5, which required every signer.
6. **Corrections to the course-level document during an offering.** None allows them, following B5; counsel may want a way.
7. **Joint ownership (FSL) covers software only.** All three drafters followed v0.5 here: documentation and other work stay with the students.
8. **Smaller choices noted in each README:** for example, B's capstone wording ("where the program allows"), C's rule that a student who leaves gets no rights in later work, and what happens if both Course Private boxes are checked (B).

## Questions for counsel

Every draft marks these with "Note for counsel":

- forming one agreement among many people who sign at different times, and electronic assent;
- the buyout's enforceability;
- students under 18;
- whether Course Staff can promise not to assert rights that Harvard may own as their employer;
- the liability limits;
- the SEAS community (R-8);
- the conditional informal exception.

C adds FERPA consent and multi-party formation, including antitrust and counterparty screening. The Student Guide's statements of law were checked mainly against search summaries, because this session couldn't open copyright.gov, govinfo.gov, otd.harvard.edu, or fsl.software. `shared/student-guide-sources.md` lists what was and wasn't verified.

## Why approach A

- **It matches how each stakeholder thinks.** Course rules live in a course document. A partner gets one agreement for its project, with every choice on a cover page, and doesn't become a party to anything with other companies.
- **It has fewer seams than B.** B's brevity for Open Science partners comes from splitting the project terms into a short form and an addendum. That creates a third document that replaces sections of the second and cites the first. A's single Project Agreement can be just as short: the generator can print only the subsection for the chosen ownership, and leave out the restricted-information and publication-review terms when they aren't chosen. That gives B's reading length without B's cross-references.
- **It avoids C's adoption risk.** C is the cleanest legally, but asking every partner to join one agreement with every student and other companies invites objections the course can't negotiate away. Its 6,000-word master also lands on every student before they choose a project.
- **What to borrow from the others:**
    - from B, the conditions for a valid electronic acknowledgment, and its shorter course-level drafting where it loses nothing;
    - from C, the reading map, and a single form shape for projects with and without a partner.

If you choose A, the next step is a second close read of the chosen draft, folding in those borrowings, before counsel.
