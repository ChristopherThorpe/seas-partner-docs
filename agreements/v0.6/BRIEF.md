# Drafting brief for v0.6

This brief governs three parallel drafts of the next version of the SEAS course project agreements. Each draft uses a different document structure, but all three carry the same decisions. The goal is a set of documents that is short, plain, and easy to follow for each stakeholder, and in which each decision is stated once, in one place.

## Read first

- `agreements/course-project-letter-agreement.md` (v0.5, tag `v0.5`): the source of detailed wording and of every decision below. Reuse its substance; do not reuse its structure.
- `agreements/decision-register.md`: the decisions in v0.5, numbered A1 to H3, with the stakeholders.
- `agreements/outline-next-version.md`: the outline for approach A, and the decisions still open.
- `agreements/v0.6/SCENARIOS.md`: situations every draft must handle.

## Decisions made since v0.5

These override v0.5 where they differ.

1. **Students on a Project are parties to each other**, as well as to Partner. Teammates enforce the terms among themselves directly; no third-party beneficiary clause is needed for teammates.
2. **Three information choices** (replacing v0.5 Options 4.1 to 4.4):
    - **Open** (default): Partner gives no restricted information. Nothing about the Project is confidential except as the course-wide rules require.
    - **Course Private**: all Partner information is Course Private Information and may be shared within the Course Community only. If Partner gives something by mistake that it identifies as confidential, students delete it when Partner asks and stop using it; uses before that notice that were permitted for Course Private Information are not a breach.
    - **Course Private and Confidential**: as above, plus Partner may mark information as Confidential Information (in writing when given, or by written confirmation within ten business days of an oral or visual disclosure). Confidential Information goes only to the signers of the Project's agreement. Course Staff see it only if they sign that agreement.
3. **IP is chosen on two dials: ownership, and public licenses.**
    - **Ownership** (pick one): **Students own** (default); **Students and Partner own jointly** (only with an FSL license); **Partner owns** (assignment).
    - **Software license** (when students own): **MIT** (default), **Apache 2.0**, **both** (dual-licensed; each recipient chooses), **none**, or **other** (named). With joint ownership: **FSL-1.1-MIT** or **FSL-1.1-ALv2**.
    - **License for everything else** (documentation, reports, data, media): **CC BY 4.0** (default), **CC BY-SA 4.0**, **none**, or **other** (named).
    - **Partner owns** means no public license unless Partner later releases the work itself.
    - When students own, Partner always gets a perpetual, non-exclusive, royalty-free license to use the Project Work Product for any purpose (v0.5 §8.2), transferable only with Partner's business, with no sublicensing without the students' consent.
    - If a choice is left blank, or more than one is checked (other than MIT and Apache together), the default applies.
4. **Open Science** means: Open information, Students own, MIT and/or Apache 2.0 for software, and CC BY 4.0 or CC BY-SA 4.0 for everything else. Open Science is the default. **Any other choice needs the Course instructor's approval in writing (email is enough)**, both at signing and for any later change. Partner certifies that approval on the form; the instructor does not sign. For a project without a partner, the students certify it, and an individual project always needs it.
5. **Publication review** (v0.5 §5.2): Partner may request it unless information is Open; it applies automatically when Partner owns. Partner gets a copy 30 days before submission; the hold lasts the Publication Hold Period stated on the form (blank means until Partner consents). Consent may not be unreasonably withheld for results that contain no Confidential Information. When Partner owns and information is Open, review does not apply; instead students give 30 days' notice before public disclosure and Partner may delay it up to 60 days to file a patent.
6. **Teammates' rights** (applies whether or not there is a partner, whenever students own the work or co-own it):
    - Each student on a team gives each teammate a perpetual, non-exclusive, royalty-free ("PNERF") license to the team's Project Work Product.
    - A student's license extends to that student's **Student Company**: a company the student founded or co-founded, or in which students on the Project together hold at least 25% of the voting equity, and (for a project with a partner) that no competitor of Partner controls.
    - Course work stays non-exclusive. What a Student Company builds afterwards is its own.
    - If teammates start separate companies, each keeps its license.
    - **Buyout ("use it or lose it")**, only where students own work that has no public license (software or other category): if one or more students' Student Company is actively developing the work, it may give written notice to a teammate who is not using the work commercially (personally or through a company). The teammate may join the company on the same terms as its other student founders within [60] days; otherwise the teammate must assign their ownership share to the company for a price. Draft both price alternatives in brackets, with a note that one is chosen before use: **[Alternative 1]** the greater of (a) what each founding student received for assigning their own rights in the work to the company and (b) [0.01]% of the company's fully diluted equity; **[Alternative 2]** [__]% of the proceeds of any sale of the company or substantially all its assets, up to $[__], paid at the sale. Also bracket **[whether the sale ends the seller's license]**; if it does, the seller keeps the right to use the work for portfolio, academic, and other noncommercial purposes. Add a note for counsel on enforceability, securities, and tax.
    - The buyout does not apply under joint ownership (FSL) or when Partner owns.
7. **Course-wide rules** bind every student and every member of the Course Staff, and protect every project, whether or not it has a partner:
    - Inadvertent disclosures (v0.5 §3.7, with the reasonable-care safe harbor in §11.9).
    - Other projects' Course Private Information stays within the Course Community and is never passed to a partner, unless **that project's partner and its students** (or, for a project without a partner, its students) consent in writing; standard exclusions apply.
    - No one shares, or uses in a project, anyone else's confidential information, **such as a current or former employer's**.
    - Course Staff contribute no IP, share no grades or education records with partners (FERPA), and see a project's Confidential Information only by signing that project's agreement. They sign in a personal capacity, not for Harvard.
    - Generative AI: Harvard accounts and tools by default; a partner's restricted information only with tools that partner approves in writing or by email.
    - Reassignment: a student may ask to move to another project, including an Open Science project or a project without a partner, and the Course Staff will honor it. Work already done on the first project stays under that project's terms, and its partner's information stays confidential.
    - Partners in the Course may enforce the rules that protect their information.
8. **Confidentiality Period**: every confidentiality duty, including for other projects' information, lasts five years after a person's End Date (the earlier of the end of the Duration and the date they stop taking part). Trade secrets stay protected while they remain trade secrets. The Enabling License (v0.5 §8.9) lasts for the Confidentiality Period.
9. **Student Guide**: one shared, non-binding guide (`agreements/v0.6/shared/student-guide.md`), which students must confirm they have read before choosing a project. Agreements may refer to it but must not depend on it.
10. **Harvard's approval** is a one-time approval of the templates for listed courses, kept on file. It is not attached to every agreement. Harvard is not a party. Its conditional informal exception and its prior rights (faculty-sponsored research, agreements with third parties) are stated where students will read them.
11. **Keep, unchanged in substance:** everything else in the decision register (sections A, C, D, E, F, G, and H), including masking Partner's name in academic disclosures, the poster and video rules, credit and co-authorship, the Enabling License, Partner Contacts and the authorized officer, end-of-project steps, export control and protected data, the release and as-is terms, Massachusetts law, Adobe Acrobat Sign, and joining later.
12. **Keep as bracketed placeholders for counsel:** the limit on liability between Partner and students (v0.5 §7.5, including whether confidentiality breaches are excepted); the gross negligence and willful misconduct carve-outs from Harvard's and Course Staff's protection (v0.5 §9.3); and who counts as the SEAS community (review note R-8). Add a short "Note for counsel" wherever a draft relies on a legal assumption that needs confirming.

## Style

- Plain English. Short sentences (under 25 words where possible) and short sections. Address the reader directly only in the Student Guide.
- Use "must" for duties, "may" for permissions, and "will" for future facts. Do not use "shall".
- Define each term once, at first use, as `"**Term**"`. Avoid defining terms used only once.
- Avoid cross-references. When one is needed, cite a section number, and check that it exists and says what you claim.
- Every list of three or more uses the Oxford comma ("students, partners, and staff").
- Bracket only real drafting choices or counsel placeholders: `[...]`. Add notes for counsel as blockquotes beginning `> **Note for counsel:**`.
- Markdown (GitHub-flavored): use `##` and `###` headings, `☐` for check boxes, and a trailing `\` for line breaks inside a block. Never put a line made only of underscores (it renders as a rule); put a label before every blank. Write email fields as "Email (ending in harvard.edu): ____".
- Forms must suit the document generator: every blank and choice is clearly labeled, and defaults are printed beside the choices.
- Do not include any AI model names or identifiers.

## What each approach delivers

In its own folder (`agreements/v0.6/approach-a/`, `approach-b/`, or `approach-c/`), as Markdown files:

1. The complete documents for the approach, ready for counsel review apart from the bracketed items.
2. `README.md` with:
    - a one-paragraph summary of the approach;
    - a table of **who signs what, and when**, for each stakeholder;
    - the approximate length of each document in words;
    - **how each scenario in `SCENARIOS.md` is handled**, one or two lines each, citing the document and section;
    - strengths, weaknesses, and open questions;
    - a list of any decisions the drafter made that this brief did not settle, with the reason.

Write only inside your approach's folder. Do not run git, and do not edit any other file.
