## 1. These rules

**1.1 Who agrees.** Every student in the Course and every member of its Course Staff agrees to these rules with each other, once per offering of the Course or on joining it later. The "**Course Staff**" are the instructors, teaching fellows, teaching assistants, and course assistants. They agree in a personal capacity, not for Harvard.

**1.2 Projects.** Each project in the Course (a "**Project**") has one or more students, who are "**teammates**", and may have a sponsoring company (a "**partner**"). Its "**Project Agreement**" is the Open Science Project Agreement (with any Restricted Project Addendum) if it has a partner, or the Team Project Form if not. A Project Agreement may add to these rules. It cannot reduce their protection of anyone who did not sign it.

"**Project Work Product**" means all work that students create in the course of a Project. It includes improvements to, and works derived from, materials that others provide, but not those materials themselves. Third-party materials keep their own licenses. The software in it is the "**Project Software**".

**1.3 Enforcement.** Everyone bound by these rules may enforce them against each other. Each partner may enforce Sections 2.1, 2.2, 2.4, and 4.3 as they protect its information or its Project. Harvard and each member of the Course Staff may enforce Section 6.3.

## 2. Rules for everyone

**2.1 Inadvertent disclosures.** Anyone who receives confidential information through the Course that was not meant for them must use reasonable care to tell the sender, not copy or use it, and delete every copy, including notes and photos. Doing this promptly is reasonable care. No one is liable for merely receiving such information, but this does not protect later intentional use or disclosure.

**2.2 Other Projects' information.** A Project Agreement may make information "**Course Private Information**", which may be shared only within the Course Community, or "**Confidential Information**", which may be shared only with that agreement's signers. The "**Course Community**" is:

- the Course's students and Course Staff;
- Harvard staff who support the Course; and
- [members of the SEAS community] and invited guests while attending a "**Private Course Event**": a Course event that is not open to the public, such as a class or a poster session.

The Course Staff must tell attendees at each Private Course Event not to record or share Course Private Information. Everyone must keep another Project's Course Private Information within the Course Community, use it only for the Course, and never give it to a partner. That Project's partner and students (or, without a partner, its students) may consent in writing to other uses.

**2.3 Others' confidential information.** No one may share in the Course, or use in a Project, confidential information that they are not authorized to share or use, such as a current or former employer's.

**2.4 Generative AI.** For Course work, everyone must use Harvard accounts and Harvard's generative AI tools, unless a partner provides its own tools. A partner's Course Private Information or Confidential Information may go only into tools that the partner approves in writing (email is enough). This includes Harvard's tools.

**2.5 Exclusions.** No confidentiality duty under these rules or any Project Agreement covers information that the recipient shows:

- is or becomes public through no breach;
- they knew without restriction before receiving it;
- came from a third party that was free to share it; or
- they developed independently, without using it.

Anyone may disclose information as law or a court requires, after prompt notice to its owner where the law allows. Nothing prevents a report to a government agency, or any other disclosure the law protects, including under 18 U.S.C. § 1833(b).

## 3. Course Staff

**3.1 Role.** The Course Staff advise, supervise, evaluate, and grade Projects. They contribute no intellectual property to any Project and claim no rights in Project Work Product. Their grades and feedback are not Project Work Product.

**3.2 Student records.** The Course Staff must not share any student's grades or other education records (20 U.S.C. § 1232g) with any partner.

**3.3 Confidential Information.** A member of the Course Staff may receive a Project's Confidential Information only by signing its Project Agreement to do so. They have no signer's duties on any other Project.

## 4. Choosing a Project

**4.1 Open Science.** A Project is "**Open Science**" if no information about it is restricted and its students own their Project Work Product. They license software under the MIT License, the Apache License 2.0, or both, and everything else under CC BY 4.0 or CC BY-SA 4.0. Open Science is the default. Any other choice needs the Course instructor's approval in writing (email is enough), at signing and for any later change.

**4.2 Before choosing and signing.** Before choosing a Project, each student must confirm that they have read the Student Guide, which explains these rules but binds no one. A student must sign a Project Agreement before receiving a partner's information or doing material work on the Project.

**4.3 Reassignment.** A student may ask to move to another Project for any reason, including to an Open Science Project or a Project without a partner. The Course Staff must honor the request, if necessary by approving an individual Project. Work the student did on the first Project stays under its Project Agreement, as if they had signed it, and they assign or license that work accordingly. They must treat the first partner's information as Course Private Information, or as Confidential Information if it was marked so. In a capstone course, a student who declines every partner Project's terms may instead do a Project without a partner or, where the program allows, write a thesis.

## 5. Teammates' rights

**5.1 Licenses.** Whenever students own their Project Work Product, alone or jointly with a partner, each student grants each teammate a perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free license to use and exploit it for any purpose. A teammate may not transfer or sublicense the license, except through a Student Company. It is subject to Section 5.3 and to the Project Agreement.

**5.2 Student Companies.** The license extends to a teammate's "**Student Company**": a company that the teammate founded or co-founded, or in which the Project's students together hold at least 25% of the voting equity when it first uses the work. For a Project with a partner, no competitor of the partner may control it. A Student Company may give its customers the rights they need to use its products. Course work stays non-exclusive, and what a Student Company builds afterward is its own. If teammates start separate companies, each company keeps its license.

**5.3 Buyout ("use it or lose it").** This section applies only to Project Work Product that students own with no public license. It never applies to software under an FSL license, or when a partner owns or co-owns the work.

(a) A Student Company that is actively developing that work may give written notice to a teammate who is not then using it commercially, whether personally or through a company.

(b) Within [60] days, the teammate may join the company on the same terms as its other student founders.

(c) Otherwise, the teammate assigns their ownership share in that work to the company when the company pays the price, or under Alternative 2 promises in writing to pay it. This section is the assignment, and the teammate must sign documents confirming it.

(d) The price is [Alternative 1: the greater of (i) what each founding student received for assigning their own rights in the work to the company (if the amounts differ, the average) and (ii) [0.01]% of the company's fully diluted equity] [Alternative 2: [__]% of the proceeds of any sale of the company or of substantially all its assets, up to $[__], paid at the sale].

(e) [The assignment ends the teammate's license under Section 5.1 for that work, except for portfolio, academic, and other noncommercial use.]

> **Note for counsel:** choose one price alternative before use. Please also confirm (1) that this conditional, self-executing assignment binds a teammate who refuses to sign, including under 17 U.S.C. § 204(a) when these rules are acknowledged electronically; (2) that "the same terms as its other student founders" is definite enough; (3) securities law, where the price or joining involves equity for a non-founder; and (4) the tax treatment of equity or deferred sale proceeds.

**5.4 Copies and credit.** Before their End Date, and before Course computing resources shut down, each student must make reasonable efforts to give each teammate copies of the Project Work Product. Anyone submitting material about Project Work Product for publication must:

- offer each teammate co-authorship, unless the material covers substantial work done after the Duration, and otherwise credit them in the acknowledgments unless they prefer anonymity;
- give co-authors reasonable time to review the draft and request changes; and
- give other teammates reasonable time to comment.

A teammate may be asked to sign a reasonable nondisclosure agreement before seeing a confidential draft.

## 6. Harvard

**6.1 Not a party.** The President and Fellows of Harvard College ("**Harvard**") and its School of Engineering and Applied Sciences ("**SEAS**") are not parties to, and have no obligations under, these rules or any Project Agreement. Harvard's approval of these templates for the Course is on file.

**6.2 Harvard's rights.** Under a conditional informal exception to its intellectual property policy, Harvard claims no rights in what students create as coursework using resources generally available to the Course. The exception does not reach Harvard's rights from faculty-sponsored research or from an agreement between Harvard and a third party. Using other Harvard resources may also take work outside it. Every student's rights are subject to Harvard's.

## 7. General

**7.1 End Date and Confidentiality Period.** A person's "**End Date**" is the earlier of the end of the Duration and the day they stop taking part in the Course or, for duties tied to one Project, in that Project. Every confidentiality duty under these rules or any Project Agreement, including for other Projects' information, lasts until five years after the person's End Date (the "**Confidentiality Period**"). A trade secret stays protected while it remains a trade secret.

**7.2 Other terms.** Massachusetts law governs, and only Massachusetts state and federal courts have jurisdiction. Each person's duties are their own. Electronic signatures and acknowledgments, including through Adobe Acrobat Sign or a course website that requires a Harvard login, are valid. Each signer confirms that they are of the age of majority. An unenforceable term does not affect the rest. A new version of these rules applies only to a later offering.
