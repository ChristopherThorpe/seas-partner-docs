## 1. These rules

**1.1 Who agrees.** Every student enrolled in the Course and every member of its Course Staff agrees to these rules, with each other, once for each offering of the Course. The "**Course Staff**" are the instructors, teaching fellows, teaching assistants, course assistants, and others who teach the Course. Anyone who enrolls or joins the Course Staff later agrees on joining. Members of the Course Staff agree in a personal capacity, not for Harvard, and do not bind Harvard.

**1.2 Projects.** Each project in the Course (a "**Project**") has one or more students and may have a sponsoring company (a "**partner**"). Every student on a Project is a "**teammate**" of the others. Each Project has one "**Project Agreement**": for a Project with a partner, the Open Science Project Agreement signed by the partner and the Project's students, with any Restricted Project Addendum; for a Project without a partner, the Team Project Form signed by its students. A Project Agreement may add to these rules for its Project. It cannot reduce the protection these rules give anyone who did not sign it.

"**Project Work Product**" means all results, reports, presentations, posters, videos, software, data, designs, and other work that students create, alone or together, in the course of a Project. It includes improvements to, and works derived from, materials that others provide, but not those materials themselves. For example, a model fine-tuned on a partner's data is Project Work Product; the data is not. Third-party and open-source materials keep their own licenses.

**1.3 Enforcement.** Anyone bound by these rules may enforce them against anyone else bound by them. Each partner may enforce Sections 2.1, 2.2, 2.4, and 4.3 as they protect its information or its Project. Harvard and each member of the Course Staff may enforce Section 6.3. No one else has rights under these rules.

## 2. Rules for everyone in the Course

**2.1 Inadvertent disclosures.** Anyone who receives someone's confidential information through the Course when it was not meant for them must use reasonable care to:

- tell the person who disclosed it, or the Project's team;
- not copy, save, forward, or otherwise use it; and
- delete or destroy every copy they have, including notes, files, messages, and photos.

This applies however it happens, for example through a misdirected email, an open shared folder, or a presentation. No one is liable for merely receiving such information, but this protection does not cover anyone who intentionally uses or discloses it afterward. Taking these steps promptly after learning of the mistake is reasonable care. "Reasonable care" means what a reasonable person would do in the recipient's position, considering their role in the Course, the nature of the information, and when they learned it was not meant for them. This section does not apply to Course Private Information shared in class.

**2.2 Other Projects' information.** A Project Agreement may make information "**Course Private Information**", which may be shared only within the Course Community, or "**Confidential Information**", which may be shared only with that agreement's signers. The "**Course Community**" is:

- students enrolled in the Course during the Duration;
- the Course Staff;
- Harvard faculty and staff who support the Course; and
- [members of the SEAS community] and invited guests attending a Private Course Event, while they attend.

A "**Private Course Event**" is a class, presentation, poster or video session, project review, or similar Course event that is not open to the public. At each one, the Course Staff must tell attendees that Course Private Information shown there must stay private and must not be photographed or recorded.

Each student and member of the Course Staff must keep another Project's Course Private Information within the Course Community, use it only for the Course, and never give it to any partner. These duties end if that Project's partner and its students (or, for a Project without a partner, its students) consent in writing.

**2.3 Other people's confidential information.** No one may share with anyone in the Course, or use in a Project, confidential information that they are not authorized to share or use, such as a current or former employer's.

**2.4 Generative AI.** Students and Course Staff must use their Harvard accounts and Harvard's generative AI tools for Course work, unless a partner asks its team to use the partner's own tools at the partner's expense. A partner's Course Private Information or Confidential Information may be used only with generative AI tools that the partner approves in writing (email is enough). This applies to Harvard's tools too.

**2.5 Exclusions and permitted disclosures.** No confidentiality duty under these rules or any Project Agreement covers information that the recipient can show:

- is or becomes public through no breach;
- they knew without restriction before receiving it;
- they received from a third party that owed no duty of confidentiality; or
- they developed independently, without using it.

Anyone may disclose information when law, regulation, subpoena, or court order requires it. Where the law allows, they must first give its owner prompt notice and reasonable help to seek a protective order, at the owner's expense. Nothing in these rules or any Project Agreement prevents anyone from reporting a possible violation of law to a government agency, or from any other disclosure the law protects. Under 18 U.S.C. § 1833(b), an individual is immune from liability under trade secret law for disclosing a trade secret in confidence to a government official or a lawyer solely to report or investigate a suspected violation of law, or in a court filing made under seal.

## 3. Course Staff

**3.1 Role.** The Course Staff advise, supervise, evaluate, and grade Projects. They contribute no intellectual property to any Project, and claim no ownership of or license to any Project Work Product because of their work for the Course. Their evaluations, grades, and feedback are not Project Work Product.

**3.2 Student records.** The Course Staff must not share any student's grades or other education records (as defined in the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act, 20 U.S.C. § 1232g) with any partner. Students may share their own work.

**3.3 Confidential Information.** A member of the Course Staff may receive a Project's Confidential Information only by signing that Project's Agreement to do so. They have no duties of a signer on any Project they have not signed for.

## 4. Choosing a Project

**4.1 Open Science.** A Project is "**Open Science**" when its information is open (no partner gives restricted information, and the team restricts none of its own), its students own their Project Work Product, and they license it publicly: under the MIT License, the Apache License 2.0, or both, for software, and under CC BY 4.0 or CC BY-SA 4.0 for everything else. Open Science is the default for every Project. Any other choice needs the Course instructor's approval in writing (email is enough), both at signing and for any later change. Before choosing a Project, each student must confirm that they have read the Student Guide. The Guide explains these rules and the choices, but it binds no one.

**4.2 Signing first.** A student must sign a Project's Agreement before receiving any partner's information or doing material work on that Project.

**4.3 Reassignment.** A student may ask to move to another Project for any reason, including declining a Project's terms, and may ask for an Open Science Project or a Project without a partner. The Course Staff will honor the request. If no other Project is available, the instructor will approve an individual Project. Any work the student did on the first Project stays under its Project Agreement, as if they had signed it on the same terms as its other students, and they assign or license that work as those terms provide. They must treat any information the first Project's partner gave them as Course Private Information, or as Confidential Information if it was marked so.

**4.4 Capstone courses.** If the Course is a capstone for a degree program, a student who declines every partner Project's terms may do a Project without a partner or, where the program allows, write a thesis instead.

## 5. Teammates' rights

**5.1 Licenses among teammates.** Whenever students own their Project Work Product, alone or jointly with a partner, each student grants each teammate a perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free license under their rights in it. The license covers using, copying, modifying, distributing, making, selling, and otherwise exploiting the work for any purpose. A teammate may not transfer or sublicense it, except through a Student Company under Section 5.2. It is subject to Section 5.3 and to the Project Agreement, including its confidentiality terms, any FSL terms, and the partner's rights in its own materials.

**5.2 Student Companies.** A teammate's license extends to their "**Student Company**": a company that the teammate founded or co-founded, or in which students on the Project together hold at least 25% of the voting equity when it first uses the work. For a Project with a partner, no competitor of the partner may control it. A Student Company may use the work as the teammate could, and may give its customers the rights they need to use its products and services. Course work stays non-exclusive: these licenses give no one exclusive rights in Project Work Product. What a Student Company builds afterward is its own. If teammates start separate companies, each company keeps its license.

**5.3 Buyout ("use it or lose it").** This section applies only to Project Work Product that students own and that has no public license, in any category. It never applies when a partner owns the work or owns it jointly with the students.

(a) *Notice.* If a Student Company of one or more teammates is actively developing that work, it may give written notice to a teammate who, on that date, is not using the work commercially, personally or through a company.

(b) *Joining.* Within [60] days after the notice, the teammate may join the company on the same terms as its other student founders.

(c) *Assignment.* If the teammate does not join, they assign their ownership share in that work to the company when the company pays the price (or, under Alternative 2, promises in writing to pay it). This section is the assignment, and the teammate must sign any document that confirms it.

**5.4 Copies and credit.** Before their End Date, and before any Course computing resources holding Project Work Product shut down, each student must make reasonable efforts to ensure that each teammate receives copies of the Project Work Product they are entitled to. Before anyone submits material about a Project's Project Work Product for publication:

- each teammate must be offered co-authorship, unless the submission covers substantial work done after the Duration, and a teammate not credited as a co-author is credited in the acknowledgments unless they ask to stay anonymous;
- each co-author must have reasonable time to review a draft and request changes; and
- each teammate must have reasonable time to review a draft and comment on it.

A teammate may be asked to sign a reasonable nondisclosure agreement before receiving a confidential draft.

## 6. Harvard

**6.1 Harvard is not a party.** The President and Fellows of Harvard College ("**Harvard**") and the Harvard John A. Paulson School of Engineering and Applied Sciences ("**SEAS**") are not parties to these rules or to any Project Agreement, and have no obligations under them. Harvard has approved the templates for these documents for the Course, and keeps that approval on file.

**6.2 Harvard's rights.** Harvard's Office of Technology Development applies a conditional informal exception to Harvard's Statement of Policy in Regard to Intellectual Property. Under it, Harvard claims no rights in intellectual property that students create as coursework using resources generally available to students in the Course. The exception does not reach rights that Harvard has from faculty-sponsored research or from an agreement between Harvard and a third party. Using other Harvard resources, such as some research computing, may also take work outside it. No Project Agreement assigns or licenses any right that Harvard owns, and every student's rights are subject to Harvard's.

## 7. Duration and general terms

**7.1 End Date and Confidentiality Period.** A person's "**End Date**" is the earlier of the end of the Duration and the date they stop taking part in the Course or, for duties tied to one Project, in that Project. Every confidentiality duty under these rules or any Project Agreement, including for other Projects' information, lasts until five years after the person's End Date (the "**Confidentiality Period**"). Confidential Information that is a trade secret stays protected for as long as it remains a trade secret. These rules otherwise continue to bind each person after their End Date.

**7.2 General.** Massachusetts law governs these rules, and the state and federal courts in Massachusetts have exclusive jurisdiction. Each person's duties are their own, and no one answers for another's breach. Electronic signatures and acknowledgments, including through Adobe Acrobat Sign or a course website that requires a Harvard login, are as effective as handwritten ones. Each signer confirms that they have reached the age of majority. If any part of these rules is unenforceable, the rest remains in effect. These rules apply for the whole Duration, and a new version applies only to a later offering.
