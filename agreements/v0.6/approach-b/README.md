# Approach B: tiered short forms

## Summary

Approach B makes the common case short and adds terms only when a partner chooses them. Every student and member of the Course Staff acknowledges one set of **Course Rules** per course offering. The Course Rules hold everything that applies to every Project: shared definitions, the course-wide confidentiality rules, the Course Staff's role, teammates' rights and the buyout, reassignment, Harvard's position, and the Confidentiality Period. A Project with a partner that stays Open Science then needs only the **Open Science Project Agreement** (the "Short Form"), whose only choices are MIT and/or Apache 2.0 and CC BY 4.0 or CC BY-SA 4.0. If the partner wants anything else, the parties also sign the **Restricted Project Addendum**. Its boxes switch on restricted information, other ownership or licenses, publication review, and Course Staff signatures, and it governs where it differs from the Short Form. Projects without a partner use the one-page **Team Project Form**. Harvard signs a one-time **Harvard Approval** of all four templates, which stays on file. Students also confirm that they have read the shared, non-binding Student Guide to Course Project Agreements.

Abbreviations used below: **CR** (Course Rules), **SF** (Open Science Project Agreement), **RA** (Restricted Project Addendum), **TPF** (Team Project Form), and **HA** (Harvard Approval). "§" means a section of that document.

### Is an electronic acknowledgment at enrollment enough?

**Yes, with conditions, which are now written into HA §1.** Under the federal ESIGN Act and the Massachusetts Uniform Electronic Transactions Act (M.G.L. c. 110G), an electronic signature has the same effect as a handwritten one if the person intends to sign. Harvard's approval covers an electronic acknowledgment only if:

- the person sees the full text of the CR;
- they take an affirmative step, such as clicking "I agree" or typing their name, after a Harvard login;
- the Course records who agreed, to which version, and when, and gives them a copy; and
- a student acknowledges before choosing a Project.

A notice in the syllabus, or "by enrolling you agree", is not enough. The CR form one agreement among everyone who acknowledges them for the offering, and each acknowledgment is a counterpart (CR §7.2). They contain present license grants and two conditional assignments (reassignment in CR §4.3 and the buyout in CR §5.3). A copyright transfer needs a signed writing (17 U.S.C. § 204(a)). An authenticated electronic signature should satisfy that, and notes for counsel at CR §4.3 and §5.3 ask counsel to confirm it. Signing the SF or TPF re-affirms the CR, which gives a second signed writing (SF §1.4, TPF opening). Adobe Acrobat Sign can be used instead (CR §7.2). A note for counsel at CR §7.2 covers students under 18. "At enrollment" in practice means the first week of the Course, through the course website, because the registrar's enrollment flow may not support a custom agreement.

## Moving from the Short Form to the addendum mid-course (scenario 12)

1. The partner gets the instructor's email approval of the new choices.
2. Partner's authorized officer and every Student then taking part sign an RA (SF §7.2). It has an Addendum Date, the new boxes checked, and the approval in box E (RA §1.2). If the Addendum Date is left blank, it is the date the last party signs (RA header).
3. From the Addendum Date (RA §1.3):
    - **Information:** the new information choice covers Partner Information given from then on. Earlier information stays open unless it is listed on the RA and is not yet public.
    - **Ownership and licenses:** the new choices reach earlier work as between the parties. Open licenses that others already hold in copies they received stay in force.
4. A Student who will not accept the change asks to be reassigned (CR §4.3). Their earlier work stays under the Open Science terms, and the change does not bind them (SF §7.2).
5. No new SF is signed. The RA governs where it differs from the SF (RA §1.1). A later RA replaces an earlier one from its own Addendum Date (RA §1.3).

## Strengths

- **The common case is short to sign.** An Open Science partner signs about 2,300 words with two license choices, and never sees the restricted-project terms.
- **Complexity is opt-in and gated.** Every RA section starts with the box that switches it on, and the RA governs where it differs from the SF.
- **Course-wide rules are signed once.** Students and Course Staff are parties to the CR with each other. Teammates and other projects' students enforce them directly. Partners, Harvard, and Student Companies get narrow rights to enforce specific sections (CR §1.3, SF §1.4).
- **Reassignment is stronger than in v0.5.** A student's work on a first Project is covered by the CR, which they acknowledged before any project work, rather than by an agreement they declined (CR §4.3).
- **Mid-course changes are mechanical.** A partner signs an RA; nothing is re-signed.
- **One model for every project.** The TPF uses the same license dials as the partner forms, so students learn one system.

## Weaknesses

- **Three documents cross-reference each other.** A partner with an RA reads the SF, the RA, and the CR parts listed in SF §1.4. Renumbering any of them breaks citations, so the templates must be versioned together (HA §1).
- **The common case is not short to read.** An Open Science partner signs about 2,300 words but relies on about 800 more in the CR (SF §1.4).
- **The Course Rules are well over target.** At about 2,190 words of rules text, they exceed the 1,000 to 1,500 aim by half. The review fixes added most of the growth: the buyout mechanics (the buyout is now about 340 words), the no-accounting rule, the definitions moved into §1.2, counterparts, and the Course Staff non-assertion rule. None could move without splitting a decision.
- **The name "Open Science Project Agreement" misleads once an RA is attached.** The SF preamble says the RA governs, but a reader skimming titles may not notice.
- **Partner is not a party to the CR.** It is bound to CR §2.1, §2.2 (for information it receives through the Course), §2.5, and §6.3 by incorporation, and enforces CR §2.1, §2.2, §2.4, and §4.3 as a third-party beneficiary (SF §1.4, CR §1.3).
- **Confidentiality is in two places:** other projects' information in the CR, and a partner's own information in the RA.
- **Electronic assent to IP transfers.** The CR's conditional assignments rest on an electronic acknowledgment, re-affirmed by signing the SF or TPF. Counsel must confirm both, and the position of students under 18.
- **The poster session gives a patent-minded partner little notice.** Partner gets its copy only when the poster is presented (SF §4.1, carried from v0.5 §5.3). In scenario 7, that may be too late.

## Open questions

- Which buyout price alternative to use, whether founders' different amounts are averaged, whether the sale ends the seller's license, and the [60]-day period (CR §5.3). Counsel's points (1) to (7) in the note there.
- Counsel placeholders: the liability limit, its confidentiality exception, and whether it should limit claims between Students at all (SF §6.3); the gross negligence carve-out and the mandatory release (CR §6.3); the SEAS community (CR §1.2, R-8).
- Whether Harvard must consent to the Course Staff's promise not to assert rights in contributions (CR §3.1 note).
- Whether partners on Partner-owned Projects should get advance copies, for example 10 business days, before Private Course Events.
- Whether checking both Course Private boxes should fall back to Open, as RA §1.1 now says under the brief's default rule, or to the more protective choice.

## Decisions made by the drafter

Decisions marked *(owner)* were confirmed by the owner after the independent review.

1. **Shared definitions live in CR §1.2 and §4.1** (Project, Project Agreement, Project Work Product, Project Software, Course Private Information, Confidential Information, Course Community, Private Course Event, End Date, Open Science, and the license names). The SF, RA, and TPF adopt them. *Reason:* the CR is the only document everyone signs, so each term is defined once.
2. **Partner is bound to CR §2.1, §2.2, §2.5, and §6.3 by incorporation,** and SF §1.4 lists every CR part Partner relies on. Other projects' partners and students, Harvard, and the Course Staff may enforce those sections against Partner (SF §1.4). *(owner, for §2.2 and the list)*. *Reason:* no duplicated terms, and every protected person has a direct right.
3. **Integration and precedence:** the SF and TPF re-affirm the CR, the SF's entire-agreement clause includes the CR, and a Project Agreement governs among its signers if it conflicts with the CR, stated once in CR §1.2. *Reason:* without this, a holdout could argue that the SF superseded the teammates' rights in CR §5.
4. **The RA always has an information box with Open as the default.** *Reason:* a partner may leave Open Science only on IP, as in the second half of scenario 7.
5. **RA box C defaults to "As checked in the Short Form"** instead of repeating the license choices. *Reason:* each choice appears once.
6. **Defaults apply per box** (RA §1.1, TPF §1). Joint ownership with neither or both FSL variants checked gives FSL-1.1-MIT. *Reason:* the earlier "per line" rule did not fit the layout; FSL-1.1-MIT is closest to the overall MIT default.
7. **Joint ownership covers only the software;** everything else stays with the students, licensed under RA box C (RA §4.2). *Reason:* this keeps v0.5 §8.1.1 (register E2) unchanged, and the FSL is a software license.
8. **The buyout never applies to a Project whose Project Agreement chooses joint ownership under the FSL, or when a partner owns the work** (CR §5.3) *(owner)*. *Reason:* the brief excludes joint ownership, and this keeps the buyout off the documentation and data of an FSL project. The text says "chooses joint ownership under the FSL", because most team work is jointly owned by copyright law, which plain "joint ownership" would have excluded.
9. **Buyout mechanics** *(owner)*: eligibility is tested on the notice date; the notice states the price and joining terms; the assignment covers all right, title, and interest on payment or tender; there is a cash alternative if equity is refused for 30 days; there is a power of attorney after 30 days; the company must first agree to be bound; and the company may enforce. *Reason:* the scenario 8 holdout will not sign anything.
10. **Founders' different amounts are averaged** in Alternative 1(i), now bracketed. *Reason:* the brief's "what each founding student received" is ambiguous when amounts differ.
11. **No accounting among teammates** (CR §5.1). *Reason:* a co-owner's duty to account comes from co-ownership, not from any license, so "royalty-free" alone did not waive it.
12. **A Student Company may give its customers the rights they need to use its products,** subject to any limits on licensing jointly owned software (CR §5.2). *Reason:* otherwise a non-sublicensable license cannot support selling software, and the FSL limits must still win.
13. **Mid-course RA rules** (RA §1.3). A blank Addendum Date means the date the last party signs *(owner)*, and a later RA replaces an earlier one. *Reason:* scenario 12. Open licenses already granted cannot be withdrawn, and information given as open should not become secret unless listed and still private.
14. **"Recipient" replaces "signer" in RA §2** and includes later joiners. *Reason:* Partner also signs the RA, so the old wording bound Partner's own data.
15. **Guests at poster and video sessions will be told not to record Course Private Information** (CR §2.2) *(owner)*. *Reason:* scenario 19, and R-8 asks whether attendees must be told. It is stated as a fact, not as a personal duty of each staff member, and does not apply to ordinary classes.
16. **Reassignment to an individual project needs the instructor's approval** (CR §4.3) *(owner)*. *Reason:* the brief preserves the instructor's discretion over individual projects.
17. **Harvard's and the Course Staff's protection covers loss from the use or disclosure of Project information or Project Work Product, or from someone else's breach.** It does not protect a staff member who intentionally breaches the CR (CR §6.3) *(owner)*. *Reason:* every student now gives this release, so it is kept narrow.
18. **Course Staff who contribute anyway will not assert rights** in the contribution (CR §3.1) *(owner)*. *Reason:* scenario 24; a TA's code would otherwise cloud title.
19. **The liability-limit placeholder no longer reaches "the Course" or claims between Students under the CR, and any party may seek an injunction for the IP terms** (SF §6.3, §6.4) *(owner)*. *Reason:* the Students are now parties to each other.
20. **Publication review covers disclosures relating to Partner Information or, if Partner owns, to Project Work Product** (RA §3.2) *(owner)*. *Reason:* this keeps v0.5 §5.2's scope when students own.
21. **Course Staff who sign the RA agree only to its confidentiality sections,** plus injunctions and general terms (RA §5). *Reason:* register E7 and F4.
22. **Electronic acknowledgment of the CR is enough,** with the conditions in HA §1; a student who does not acknowledge may not take part *(owner)*; the CR are one agreement in counterparts (CR §7.2). *Reason:* see above.
23. **The reasonable-care standard is restated in one sentence** (CR §2.1) *(owner)*. *Reason:* v0.5 §11.9's standard had been dropped.
24. **On the TPF, one signer means an individual project;** the project-type box is gone *(owner)*. *Reason:* the box was redundant.
25. **The TPF repeats the FSL mechanics in short form** rather than citing the RA *(owner)*. *Reason:* TPF readers should not need the RA.
26. **The CR change only by a new version for a later offering** (CR §7.2). *Reason:* every student and staff member is a party, so a mid-course amendment is impractical.
27. **Copyright notice fill-ins are described in words, not brackets.** *Reason:* the brief reserves brackets for drafting choices and counsel placeholders.
