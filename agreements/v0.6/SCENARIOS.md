# Scenarios for testing v0.6 drafts

Each draft's README explains how it handles every scenario below, citing the document and section. Reviewers check that the documents actually give that answer.

## Projects with a partner

1. **Defaults.** A four-student team with a partner; the partner checks nothing. What applies, and who signs what?
2. **Course Private, open licenses.** The partner wants its data kept within the Course but is happy with MIT and CC BY 4.0. What approval is needed, and how is it shown?
3. **A TA needs Confidential Information.** Information is Course Private and Confidential. One TA must see Confidential Information to grade the work; another TA helps the team but doesn't need it.
4. **Closed code, open paper.** Students own; software has no public license; documentation is CC BY 4.0. What can the partner do with the code, and can the students publish a paper?
5. **Dual-licensed software.** Students own; software is MIT and Apache 2.0; documentation is CC BY-SA 4.0.
6. **FSL with a startup.** Joint ownership with FSL-1.1-MIT. Two of four students start a company that competes with nobody but uses the code; the partner keeps using it too. Two years later the code becomes MIT.
7. **Assignment and a patent.** The partner owns. A student wants to show the invention at the poster session. Information is Course Private. What happens with publication review and the hold? What if information were Open instead?
8. **Holdout teammate.** Students own; no public license. Three students form a company and want to raise money; the fourth has taken a job elsewhere and won't sign anything. How does the buyout work, and what does the fourth student get?
9. **Two startups.** Two teammates start separate companies using the same course work.
10. **Late joiner and early leaver.** A student joins in week 5. Another withdraws from the Course in week 8. What does each owe, and what happens to the leaver's contributions?
11. **Reassignment.** After two weeks on a project, a student declines its terms and asks to move to an Open Science project.
12. **Change of mind.** Midway through, the partner wants to move from Open to Course Private, and from student ownership to assignment.
13. **New Partner Contact; partner acquired.** The partner replaces a Partner Contact, then is acquired by another company.
14. **Demonstrations after the Course.** A student wants to demo the project to employers next year using a small sample of the partner's data.
15. **Generative AI.** A student wants to paste the partner's Course Private data into a chatbot to debug code.

## Projects without a partner

16. **Individual project, keep all rights.** A student works alone and wants to keep all rights.
17. **Team project with FSL.** A three-student team wants FSL-1.1-ALv2 and chooses one student to manage releases.
18. **Team project, Course Private.** A team wants its own work kept within the Course.

## Course-wide

19. **Inadvertent disclosure.** A student opens a shared folder and sees another team's Confidential Information. At the poster session, a visitor photographs a Course Private poster.
20. **Employer's information.** A student who works part-time wants to reuse code from their employer, and another student wants to reuse code from a former employer.
21. **Capstone student.** A capstone student declines every partner project's terms.
22. **Harvard has rights.** The Course is funded under an agreement between Harvard and a third party.
23. **Two-term course.** The Course runs as 101a and 101b across fall and spring, with a J-Term session.
24. **Course Staff.** A TA wants to contribute code to a project. An instructor is asked by a partner for students' grades.
